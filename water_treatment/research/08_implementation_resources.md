## 1. WaterTAP: the anchor open-source package for UF/RO physics + costing

**What it is.** WaterTAP is an open-source Python library of water treatment process models built on the IDAES Platform, funded by the National Alliance for Water Innovation (NAWI) under the U.S. DOE Advanced Manufacturing Office (NAWI/LBNL, 2026, https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/stable/ and https://github.com/watertap-org/watertap). The GitHub repo shows 104 stars, 93 forks, and 3,555 commits, with a graphical "Flowsheet Processor" UI, Binder-hosted Jupyter tutorials, and support contacts at watertap-support@lbl.gov (watertap-org, 2026, https://github.com/watertap-org/watertap).

**Membrane unit models available** (WaterTAP docs, 2026, https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/stable/technical_reference/unit_models/index.html): Reverse Osmosis 0D and 1D; Osmotically Assisted Reverse Osmosis (OARO) 0D and 1D; Nanofiltration (zero-order, 0D, and a Donnan-Steric-Pore-Model-with-Dielectric-Exclusion 0D variant); Membrane Distillation 0D/1D; Electrodialysis 0D/1D and bipolar ED 1D; plus pump, energy recovery device, pressure exchanger, ion exchange, GAC, UV-AOP, crystallizer, MVC, and a large set of "zero-order" (performance-curve) models. Ultrafiltration is covered primarily via zero-order models (recovery/removal fractions + energy intensity), not a mechanistic pore-blocking model — this is the main gap you will fill with your own fouling dynamics (Section 8).

**RO0D governing equations** (WaterTAP docs, 2026, https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/stable/technical_reference/unit_models/reverse_osmosis_0D.html). The model is steady-state, single liquid phase, with two selectable transport models:

- Solution-diffusion (default):

$$J_w = \rho_w \, A \,\big[(P_f - P_p) - (\pi_f - \pi_p)\big], \qquad J_s = B\,(C_{f,m} - C_p)$$

- Spiegler–Kedem–Katchalsky (SKK), adding the reflection coefficient $\sigma$:

$$J_w = \rho_w A \big[(P_f-P_p) - \sigma(\pi_f-\pi_p)\big], \qquad J_s = B(C_{f,m}-C_p) + (1-\sigma)\,\frac{J_w}{\rho_w}\,C_f$$

- Concentration polarization by film theory (option `calculated`), where the membrane-wall concentration follows:

$$C_{interface} = C_{bulk}\exp\!\left(\frac{J_w}{k_f}\right) - \frac{J_s}{J_w}\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{J_w}{k_f}\right)-1\right]$$

- Mass-transfer coefficient from a Sherwood correlation for spacer-filled channels:

$$Sh = 0.46\,(Re\,Sc)^{0.36}, \qquad k_f = \frac{D\,Sh}{d_h}$$

- Pressure drop options (fixed per stage, fixed per length, or calculated) with friction factors $f = 0.42 + 189.3/Re$ (flat-plate) or $f = 6.23\,Re^{-0.3}$ (spiral-wound) and $\frac{dP}{dx} = \frac{f \rho v_f^2}{2 d_h}$.

Degrees of freedom: minimum 4 (water permeability $A$, salt permeability $B$, permeate pressure, membrane area); +3 with calculated CP/mass transfer (spacer porosity, channel height, membrane length or width or Re). Typical seawater membrane values used throughout the WaterTAP examples are $A \approx 4.2\times10^{-12}$ m/(s·Pa) and $B \approx 3.5\times10^{-8}$ m/s (order-of-magnitude anchors for your simulator). Module geometry can be flat-sheet or spiral-wound; the RO 1D model discretizes along the flow path for staged/LSRRO work.

**Costing framework** (WaterTAP docs, 2026, https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/stable/technical_reference/costing/costing_base.html). WaterTAPCosting extends IDAES process costing; unit models self-register costing methods; indirect capital uses TIC or TPEC global multipliers. Default economics (2018 USD): plant utilization factor 90%; electricity $0.07/kWh; WACC 9.30734%; plant lifetime 30 yr; TPEC multiplier 4.121212; TIC multiplier 2.0; electrical carbon intensity 0.475 kg CO₂/kWh. Capital recovery factor:

$$f_{crf} = \frac{f_{wacc}(1+f_{wacc})^{L}}{(1+f_{wacc})^{L}-1} \approx 0.0993 \;\text{at 9.3\%, 30 yr}$$

Levelized cost of water:

$$LCOW = \frac{f_{crf}\,C_{cap,tot} + C_{op,tot}}{f_{util}\,Q}$$

with $C_{op,tot} = C_{op,fix} + C_{op,var}$, and specific energy consumption $SEC = C_{el,tot}/Q$. RO-specific costing defaults (WaterTAP docs, 2026, https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/stable/technical_reference/costing/reverse_osmosis.html): membrane capital cost $30/m² (standard) or $75/m² (high-pressure RO), membrane replacement factor 0.2 yr⁻¹, capital $= A_{mem}\cdot C_{mem}$, annual membrane O&M $= 0.2\,C_{mem}A_{mem}$; RO electricity is costed through the pump unit model. These defaults are exactly the numbers you need for the "% chemical/energy savings → $ savings → payback" arithmetic in an optimizer.

**Prebuilt flowsheets** you can copy (watertap-org, 2026, https://github.com/watertap-org/watertap/tree/main/watertap/flowsheets): `RO_with_energy_recovery`, `seawater_RO_desalination`, `lsrro` (low-salt-rejection RO cascades), `oaro`, `mvc`, `electrodialysis`, `nf_dspmde`, `gac`, `ion_exchange`, `full_water_resource_recovery_facility`, `flex_desal`, `generic_desalination_train`, and more. Tutorials ship as Jupyter notebooks (index at https://nbviewer.org/github/watertap-org/watertap/blob/main/tutorials/00-index.ipynb) runnable on Binder without installation (WaterTAP docs, 2026, https://watertap.readthedocs.io/en/stable/tutorials/index.html).

**Extensions.** `watertap-reflo` extends WaterTAP for renewable-energy-driven desalination and flexible-load operation co-optimization (solar/thermal + RO/MD); Python 3.9–3.11, pytest/Black tooling, docs on ReadTheDocs (watertap-org, 2026, https://github.com/watertap-org/watertap-reflo). WaterTAP also ships an OLI Cloud API client (`watertap/tools/oli_api`: `client.py`, `credentials.py`, `flash.py`, tests) so flowsheets can pull rigorous electrolyte properties/scaling tendencies from OLI's cloud service (watertap-org, 2026, https://github.com/watertap-org/watertap/tree/main/watertap/tools/oli_api).

## 2. IDAES-PSE and Pyomo: the equation-oriented substrate

**IDAES-PSE** (v2.12.0 at fetch time) is the DOE process systems engineering platform beneath WaterTAP: flowsheets, property packages, unit models, and solver tooling, all compiled to Pyomo. Capabilities directly relevant to this project: parameter estimation, optimization under uncertainty, ML surrogate integration (Keras surrogates, OMLT, PySMO surrogate toolbox), and a model diagnostics toolbox for debugging ill-conditioned NLPs. Developed by NETL/LBNL/Sandia + CMU, WVU, Notre Dame, Georgia Tech (IDAES, 2026, https://idaes-pse.readthedocs.io/en/stable/).

**Pyomo** is the algebraic modeling language: LP/QP/NLP/MILP/MINLP, stochastic programming, bilevel, MPEC, and DAEs via `pyomo.dae` (automatic collocation discretization); developed at Sandia, a COIN-OR project (Pyomo, 2026, https://www.pyomo.org/about). The workhorse solver for WaterTAP flowsheets is IPOPT (WaterTAP ships a patched `ipopt-watertap`). Practical import: a calibrated WaterTAP RO flowsheet **is already a Pyomo NLP**, so "setpoint optimization" is just: unfix decision variables (feed pressure, recovery, flows), add an objective (SEC or LCOW), and solve — no separate optimization framework needed.

**OMLT** (Optimization and Machine Learning Toolkit, Imperial/Sandia) closes the loop between ML and Pyomo: it embeds trained neural networks (Keras/ONNX) and gradient-boosted trees into Pyomo models as full-space, reduced-space, or ReLU-MILP formulations, with input/output scaling and bounds handling (cog-imperial, 2026, https://github.com/cog-imperial/OMLT; cite Ceccon et al., JMLR 2022). This is the canonical way to put your learned fouling model *inside* the physics-based optimizer.

## 3. MPC frameworks: GEKKO, do-mpc, CasADi

**GEKKO** (BYU, Beal/Hedengren et al., Processes 2018, https://gekko.readthedocs.io/en/latest/) is a Python package for "machine learning and optimization of mixed-integer and differential algebraic equations", interfacing APMonitor with APOPT/IPOPT/BPOPT. Its `IMODE` switch covers steady-state simulation, parameter regression/data reconciliation, real-time optimization (RTO), dynamic simulation, moving horizon estimation (MHE), and nonlinear MPC in one model definition — the fastest path to a working RO NMPC prototype. It also has a small `brain` deep-learning module so a fitted NN can serve as the MPC internal model. `pip install gekko`.

**do-mpc** (TU Dortmund, https://www.do-mpc.com/en/latest/; Fiedler et al., Control Engineering Practice, 2023) is a modular toolbox for robust NMPC and MHE on nonlinear/DAE systems: separate `model`, `simulator`, `estimator`, `controller` classes; orthogonal collocation on finite elements; and **robust multi-stage MPC** that branches scenarios over parametric uncertainty (e.g., ±10% in a fouling-rate parameter) — a natural fit for RO where the membrane permeability $A(t)$ is uncertain and drifting. Built on CasADi.

**CasADi** (https://web.casadi.org/; Andersson et al., Mathematical Programming Computation, 2019) is the open-source symbolic/algorithmic-differentiation framework both do-mpc and many custom NMPC codes are built on: Python/MATLAB/C++ front ends; interfaces to IPOPT and BONMIN (NLP/MINLP), CPLEX/GUROBI (QP), and SUNDIALS CVODES/IDAS integrators; designed for trajectory optimization, optimal control, and real-time NMPC.

**Decision rule.** Steady-state/economic setpoint optimization and design → Pyomo/WaterTAP (+OMLT for ML constraints). Dynamic control with horizons of minutes–hours → do-mpc (rigorous, robust-MPC) or GEKKO (fastest to first result). CasADi directly → only if you need custom NMPC transcription or maximum runtime speed.

**MPC-on-RO evidence base** (all located via Crossref, 2026, https://api.crossref.org/): Abbas (2006), "Model predictive control of a reverse osmosis desalination unit," *Desalination*, DOI 10.1016/j.desal.2005.10.033 — the early DMC-style demonstration on a lumped RO model. Bartman, McFall, Christofides & Cohen (2009), "Model-predictive control of feed flow reversal in a reverse osmosis desalination process," *J. Process Control*, DOI 10.1016/j.jprocont.2008.06.016 — NMPC executing feed-flow reversal maneuvers on the UCLA experimental RO plant. Bartman, Zhu, Christofides & Cohen (2010), "Minimizing energy consumption in reverse osmosis membrane desalination using optimization-based control," *J. Process Control*, DOI 10.1016/j.jprocont.2010.09.004 — real-time SEC minimization over feed pressure/flow on the same rig; the seminal template for the "setpoint optimizer" layer. Kargar & Mehrad (2020), robust MPC under actuator fault, *Water Supply*, DOI 10.2166/ws.2020.043. Mito et al. (2022), "Variable operation of a renewable energy-driven reverse osmosis system using model predictive control and variable recovery," *Desalination*, DOI 10.1016/j.desal.2022.115715 — MPC tracking a variable wind-power budget by manipulating recovery. Esfahani et al. (2016) benchmark MPC vs PID on RO, *Desalination and Water Treatment*, DOI 10.1080/19443994.2016.1191776.

**Deep-learning MPC with numbers.** Karimanzira & Rauschenbach (2020), "Deep Learning Based Model Predictive Control for a Reverse Osmosis Desalination Plant," *J. Applied Mathematics and Physics*, DOI 10.4236/jamp.2020.812201 (open access, https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=104606): an LSTM serves as the NMPC internal model; manipulated variable = feed pressure; outputs = permeate flow, permeate concentration, cumulative permeate quantity/concentration; feed concentration excited with Gaussian noise for robustness. Validation MAE losses: 0.030 (permeate flow), 0.0355 (permeate concentration), 0.0052 (total permeate flow), 0.0039 (total permeate concentration); setpoint-tracking performance index I = 98.7% ("almost offset-free"); 10-epoch training took 1.45 s and inference on 1,871 test points ≈0.02 s — i.e., LSTM-NMPC is computationally trivial at RO time scales.

## 4. Vendor membrane projection software: capabilities and scriptability

- **DuPont WAVE** (Water Application Value Engine) integrates **UF + RO + IX** design in one free Windows application (registration required): true mass-balance calculations with temperature/composition dependence, internally-staged RO design with per-vessel element selection, concentrate recycle at multiple levels, UF backwash/mini-CIP/CIP protocol modeling, and pressure-drop reporting. It supports **batch modeling with simultaneous batch-case creation** inside the GUI, and there is a cloud successor, **WAVE PRO** (UF/IX/RO/NF). The product pages document **no API, no scripting interface** (DuPont, 2026, https://www.dupont.com/water/resources/design-software.html).
- **Hydranautics IMSDesign-Cloud**: browser-based RO/NF/UF projections (Windows/macOS/Android), multi-pass hybrid designs up to 6 stages, chemical consumption modeling, 7 languages; companion desktop tool **RODataXL** for RO data normalization from operating plants. No API or automation mentioned (Hydranautics/Nitto, 2026, https://membranes.com/solutions/software/).
- **Toray**: free RO design software (the Toray design-system lineage, "DS2") for "membrane selection and optimal process design" plus data collection/normalization, and separate free **UF** and **MBR** module-count/normalization tools. No automation capability documented (Toray, 2026, https://www.water.toray/knowledge/tool/).
- **LG Chem Q+**: LG Water Solutions' free RO projection package; the vendor site was region-blocked during this research (https://www.lgwatersolutions.com/en/tools/q-plus-projection-software returned an LG Chem block page, 2026), and no public API is documented anywhere.

**Bottom line for automation:** none of the four mainstream projection tools exposes a supported API or CLI. Options, in order of preference: (1) reimplement the projection physics in WaterTAP/Pyomo (the RO0D equations in Section 1 are the same solution-diffusion + film-theory core the vendor tools use) and validate a handful of cases manually against WAVE/IMSDesign; (2) drive the desktop GUIs with UI automation (pywinauto/AutoHotkey) for batch dataset generation — brittle, license-gray, last resort; (3) use WAVE's in-GUI batch-case feature to export case tables manually for validation sets. Treat vendor tools as *validation oracles*, not pipeline components.

## 5. Electrolyte chemistry: OLI and open alternatives

**OLI Systems** is the commercial standard for electrolyte thermodynamics: OLI Studio (Stream Analyzer speciation, ScaleChem scaling, Corrosion Analyzer), OLI Flowsheet ESP, OLI Engine embeddings into Aspen/gPROMS, and **OLI Cloud APIs** for enterprise automation; predictions include scaling risk, speciation, pH, conductivity, and osmotic pressure, validated over 50+ years and used by 500+ companies (OLI Systems, 2026, https://www.olisystems.com/). WaterTAP's `oli_api` client (Section 1) gives you programmatic access from Python if you hold OLI Cloud credentials.

**Open-source substitutes** adequate for a from-scratch build:
- **PHREEQC** (USGS) via **phreeqpython** — full speciation/saturation-index engine; used by **ROSSpy** (below) with the Pitzer database for RO brine chemistry.
- **pyEQL** (Kingsbury Lab, LGPL-3.0, https://github.com/KingsburyLab/pyEQL): a `Solution` class computing activity/diffusion coefficients, density, conductivity, and **osmotic pressure**, with Pitzer-model support and "graceful decay" to simpler models when parameters are missing; built on pint + pymatgen + phreeqpython + IAPWS. This is the pragmatic choice for feedwater-chemistry features (ionic strength, SI for CaCO₃/CaSO₄/BaSO₄, osmotic pressure) in your ML feature vector without an OLI license.

## 6. Public datasets usable for membrane ML

Open, membrane-specific plant SCADA data is genuinely scarce; these are the usable options found:

1. **SWaT — Secure Water Treatment (iTrust, SUTD)**. A fully instrumented 6-stage water treatment testbed whose stages include chemical dosing, **ultrafiltration (P3)**, UV dechlorination, and **reverse osmosis (P5)**. The canonical dataset covers **11 days of continuous operation (7 normal + 4 under attack), 51 sensors/actuators logged at 1 Hz (~946,700 rows), with 36 labeled attack scenarios** (Goh et al., "A Dataset to Support Research in the Design of Secure Water Treatment Systems," CRITIS 2016; distribution and request form at iTrust, 2026, https://www.sutd.edu.sg/itrust/itrust-labs/datasets/ — free, "as-is", ~3 working days processing). Companion datasets: **WADI** (water distribution, 123 tags, 16 days) and **BATADAL** (attack-detection benchmark). Although built for cybersecurity, SWaT is the best open multivariate UF+RO time series for practicing soft sensors, drift detection, and anomaly detection.
2. **UCI "Water Treatment Plant" dataset** — 527 daily records × 38 sensor features from an urban wastewater plant (classic, small, good for pipeline smoke tests; UCI ML Repository, https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/106/water+treatment+plant).
3. **US Bureau of Reclamation**: the **DWPR program** (Desalination and Water Purification Research) funds lab/pilot desal studies ($1.7–2.2M/yr, 2022–2024 cycles) and publishes a numbered technical report series with pilot performance tables (USBR, 2026, https://www.usbr.gov/research/dwpr/); Reclamation's **RISE open-data portal** provides API-accessible (incl. OGC-EDR) water and water-quality time series (USBR, 2026, https://data.usbr.gov/). DWPR reports are PDFs — expect table extraction (camelot/tabula), not CSVs.
4. **Kaggle**: search results are JS-rendered (not enumerable here), but the well-known holdings are water-chemistry classification sets, e.g. Water Potability (3,276 samples, 9 features, https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/adityakadiwal/water-potability) — useful only for toy classification, **not** membrane dynamics.
5. **Paper supplementary data / described-but-private plant data** — the norm in this field: Karimanzira & Rauschenbach's models were trained on ~2 years of real RO plant operation (Section 7); Khaled et al.'s UF prognostics used **12,528 operational cycles from an industrial-scale UF system** feeding an RO plant (arXiv, 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00659). Neither dataset is public — which is precisely why Section 8's synthetic simulator is on the critical path.
6. **Seminal open-methodology anchor**: Delgrange & Cabassud et al. (1998), "Neural networks for prediction of ultrafiltration transmembrane pressure — application to drinking water production," *J. Membrane Science*, DOI 10.1016/s0376-7388(98)00217-8 — the original UF-TMP ANN soft sensor the modern literature descends from.

## 7. ML-for-membranes: architectures, features, and quantitative results

- **Karimanzira & Rauschenbach (2021)**, "Performance Prediction of a Reverse Osmosis Desalination System Using Machine Learning," *J. Geoscience and Environment Protection*, DOI 10.4236/gep.2021.97004 (open access, https://www.scirp.org/journal/paperinformation?paperid=110684). Architecture: **Multivariate Temporal Convolutional Network (MTCN)** — CNN feature extraction + TCN dilated temporal convolutions. Inputs in four blocks: membrane characteristics (hydrophobicity, charge, pore size, spacer thickness), feed quality (TDS, temperature), operations (flow, pressure, feed temperature), and **maintenance covariates (time since last cleaning, number of cleanings)** — the covariate design worth copying. Data: ~2 years of RO plant operation (35–38% recovery, constant-flow mode) spanning two CIP cycles; split 50% train (year 1)/25% val/25% test. Training: Adam, batch 128, batch-norm, 50% dropout, early stop at 21–50 epochs. Results: **RMSE 0.023 (normalized differential pressure), 0.012 (permeate TDS), 0.007 (feed pressure); correlation ρ = 0.96–0.99**.
- **Karimanzira & Rauschenbach (2020)** LSTM-NMPC — details and metrics in Section 3 (MAE 0.0039–0.0355; tracking index 98.7%).
- **Khaled, Genga & Kaymak (2026)**, "Predictive Maintenance for Ultrafiltration Membranes Using Explainable Similarity-Based Prognostics" (arXiv:2602.00659, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00659). Method: physics-informed **Health Index from TMP, flux, and resistance**; degradation trajectories fuzzified with Gaussian membership functions; RUL from Takagi–Sugeno similarity-weighted rules over historical exemplars. Data: 12,528 industrial UF cycles. Result: **RUL MAE = 4.50 cycles** — a strong, interpretable baseline to beat before reaching for deep sequence models.
- **Mohammed, Alshraideh & Alsuwaidi (2024)**, "A holistic framework for improving the prediction of reverse osmosis membrane performance using machine learning," *Desalination* 574, DOI 10.1016/j.desal.2023.117253 — tree-ensemble-centric (Random Forest/XGBoost/CatBoost per Crossref metadata) framework for RO performance prediction (Crossref, 2026, https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1016/j.desal.2023.117253).
- Other located ANN-fouling anchors: Liu, Kim & Lee (2009) ANN microfiltration fouling, *Sep. Purif. Technol.*, DOI 10.1016/j.seppur.2009.08.017; Shanmathi et al. (2025) ANN + Hermia hybrid for UF flux decline, *Chem. Eng. Res. Des.*, DOI 10.1016/j.cherd.2025.06.043.
- **Energy bounds to calibrate savings claims**: the thermodynamic minimum for 35 g/L seawater at 50% recovery is **1.06 kWh/m³**, state-of-the-art SWRO RO stages run ~2.0 kWh/m³ and whole plants ~3–4 kWh/m³ (Elimelech & Phillip, *Science* 333:712–717, 2011, DOI 10.1126/science.1200488) — so a realistic optimizer target is single-digit-to-~15% SEC reduction against a mediocre baseline, not 50%.

**GitHub repositories worth cloning** (GitHub search, 2026, https://github.com/search?q=reverse+osmosis+simulation&type=repositories):

## 8. Building a synthetic-data simulator when plant data is unavailable

The literature's private datasets (Section 6) force this step, and it is entirely tractable because RO steady-state physics is closed-form given $(A, B, k_f)$. Recipe:

**8.1 Deterministic core (steady state).** Implement Section 1's equations per time step: van 't Hoff osmotic pressure $\pi = i\,\phi\,c\,R\,T$ (≈28 bar for 35 g/L seawater; or call pyEQL for nonideal $\pi$), film-theory CP with $Sh = 0.46(ReSc)^{0.36}$, solution-diffusion fluxes, mass balances over N series elements (reuse WaterTAP RO1D, or 20 lines of NumPy with `scipy.optimize.brentq` per element). Add pump/ERD energy: $SEC = \dfrac{P_f Q_f - \eta_{ERD} P_c Q_c}{\eta_{pump} Q_p}$.

**8.2 Fouling drift (slow states).** Superimpose ODEs on the membrane parameters:
- RO permeability decline: $A(t) = A_\infty + (A_0 - A_\infty)e^{-t/\tau_f}$ with $\tau_f$ ~ weeks, plus a flux-dependent cake term $\frac{dR_c}{dt} = k_c\,J_w\,c_{foulant} - k_r R_c$ entering as $J_w = \Delta P_{net}/[\mu (R_m + R_c)]$;
- salt passage aging: $B(t) = B_0(1 + \beta t)$ (β ~ 5–15%/yr against the 20%/yr replacement economics of Section 1);
- UF cycles: Darcy resistance-in-series $J = \frac{TMP}{\mu (R_m + R_f)}$ with Hermia blocking laws $\frac{d^2t}{dV^2} = k\left(\frac{dt}{dV}\right)^n$, n = 2 (complete), 1.5 (standard), 1 (intermediate), 0 (cake) (Hermia, Trans IChemE 60:183–187, 1982); backwash recovers a fraction $\eta_{bw} \in [0.8, 0.98]$ of $R_f$, CIP resets it near-fully with a small permanent residual — this generates the sawtooth TMP trajectories the UF-prognostics paper's Health Index is built on (Khaled et al., 2026, arXiv:2602.00659).
- Scaling events: trigger accelerated decline when PHREEQC/pyEQL saturation index of CaCO₃/CaSO₄ at the wall (use CP-corrected concentration) exceeds 0; ROSSpy shows the full 1D reactive-transport version (Freiburger et al., https://github.com/freiburgermsu/ROSSpy).

**8.3 Exogenous drivers + noise.** Feed temperature: seasonal sinusoid + AR(1) weather noise (viscosity and $A$ correction ~3%/°C); feed TDS/turbidity: AR(1) with storm spikes; setpoint schedule: operator-like step changes. Sensor layer: multiplicative Gaussian noise (0.5–2% per tag), slow sensor drift (recalibrated at random intervals), stuck-at and dropout faults at realistic rates, 1-min sampling. The Gaussian-noise excitation trick on feed concentration is exactly what Karimanzira & Rauschenbach (2020) used to robustify their LSTM (DOI 10.4236/jamp.2020.812201).

**8.4 Validation.** Cross-check ~10 steady-state cases against WAVE/IMSDesign projections (Section 4) and against WaterTAP's `RO_with_energy_recovery` flowsheet; check SEC lands in the 2–4 kWh/m³ envelope (Elimelech & Phillip, 2011). Then generate 1–3 synthetic "plant-years" per scenario — matching the ~2-year scale of the real datasets in Section 7.

## 9. Recommended from-scratch build sequence

**Step 0 — Environment.** `conda create -n water python=3.11; pip install watertap` (pulls idaes-pse + pyomo; run `idaes get-extensions` for IPOPT), plus `gekko do-mpc pyequilib... pyEQL pysindy deepxde darts xgboost scikit-learn`.

**Step 1 — Simulator (weeks 1–3).** Build Section 8's simulator two ways: (a) NumPy/SciPy standalone for speed (thousands of runs/min for ML data generation); (b) the same steady-state physics as a WaterTAP/Pyomo flowsheet (`RO_with_energy_recovery` fork) for the optimizer. Calibrate both to the same $(A, B)$ and verify they agree to <1%. Libraries: NumPy, SciPy, WaterTAP, pyEQL/phreeqpython, ROSSpy (scaling scenarios).

**Step 2 — Soft sensor (weeks 3–5).** Targets: permeate TDS (from conductivity), normalized permeability/net driving pressure, CP modulus — quantities plants don't measure directly. Features: pressures, flows, temperatures, conductivities + physics features (computed $\pi$, $Re$, temperature-corrected flux). Models: Ridge → XGBoost → small MLP; benchmark against ASTM-style normalization formulas your simulator makes exact. Success bar from the literature: ρ ≥ 0.96, normalized RMSE ≤ 0.02 (Karimanzira 2021). Libraries: scikit-learn, XGBoost, SHAP.

**Step 3 — Fouling forecaster (weeks 5–8).** Task A (short horizon): forecast TMP/permeability 1–14 days ahead with planned flux and CIP schedule as **future covariates** — darts TFT/TiDE or TCN, probabilistic quantile outputs, rolling-origin backtesting. Task B (RUL): cycles-to-CIP-threshold via the Health-Index + similarity baseline (beat MAE ≈ 4.5 cycles; Khaled 2026) before trying LSTMs. Task C (structure discovery): PySINDy on the simulator's fouling states to confirm you can recover the generating ODE from noisy data; optionally DeepXDE PINN inversion for $A(t)$. Libraries: darts, PyTorch, pysindy, deepxde.

**Step 4 — Setpoint optimizer (weeks 8–11).** Steady-state economic optimization on the WaterTAP flowsheet: decision variables = feed pressure, recovery, (staged) flux split; objective = SEC or full LCOW with WaterTAP's costing ($0.07/kWh, $30/m², 0.2/yr replacement, CRF ≈ 0.0993); constraints = permeate TDS spec, max flux (fouling-safe ceiling from Step 3's model via OMLT embedding), min brine flow. Then dynamic: GEKKO (IMODE=6) or do-mpc NMPC that tracks the economic setpoints while respecting ramp limits, with the fouling state as a slowly-varying estimated parameter (MHE, IMODE=5 / do-mpc estimator); replicate the Bartman et al. (2010) SEC-minimization experiment on your simulator and quantify %-savings against a fixed-setpoint baseline. Libraries: Pyomo/WaterTAP + IPOPT, OMLT, GEKKO or do-mpc (CasADi under the hood).

**Step 5 — Advisory dashboard (weeks 11–13).** Read-only advisory (not closed-loop) is the right first deployment: current normalized KPIs (soft sensor), TMP/permeability forecast fan charts, days-to-CIP, recommended setpoints with predicted ΔSEC/Δ$ and payback arithmetic from WaterTAP costing, and drift/anomaly flags (river of residuals between soft sensor and physics model — practice the anomaly workflow on SWaT). Libraries: Streamlit or Plotly Dash, Plotly, APScheduler for the batch loop; SQLite/Parquet for the historian; MLflow for model registry.

Each step produces a testable artifact against the simulator's known ground truth — the property that makes the from-scratch path tractable without proprietary plant data.

## Implementation notes

**Data you need (minimum viable):** per-train time series at ≥1-min resolution of feed/interstage/concentrate pressures, feed/permeate/concentrate flows, feed & permeate conductivity, feed temperature, plus event logs (backwash, CIP, chemical dosing, membrane replacement) and feedwater chemistry panels (ion analysis) at whatever cadence exists. If you have no plant: the Section 8 simulator is the substitute, with SWaT (UF+RO testbed, 51 tags, 1 Hz, ~946k rows; https://www.sutd.edu.sg/itrust/itrust-labs/datasets/) as the free real-world sanity check and USBR DWPR pilot reports (https://www.usbr.gov/research/dwpr/) for realistic operating envelopes.

**Math you must actually know:** solution-diffusion transport ($J_w = A(\Delta P - \Delta\pi)$, $J_s = B\Delta C$; Wijmans & Baker, J. Membr. Sci. 107:1–21, 1995), film-theory concentration polarization and Sherwood correlations, van 't Hoff/Pitzer osmotic pressure, Darcy resistance-in-series and Hermia blocking laws for UF, pump/ERD energy balances, NLP basics (KKT, why IPOPT wants smooth models and good scaling), discrete-time MPC/MHE formulations, and time-series validation discipline (rolling-origin backtests, no random splits on autocorrelated data).

**Tools (all free except where noted):** WaterTAP + IDAES + Pyomo + IPOPT (physics, costing, steady-state optimization); GEKKO or do-mpc/CasADi (MPC/MHE); pyEQL + phreeqpython/PHREEQC, ROSSpy (chemistry/scaling; OLI Cloud API is the commercial upgrade via WaterTAP's built-in client); scikit-learn/XGBoost/PyTorch (models); darts or sktime (forecasting + covariates + backtesting); PySINDy/DeepXDE (equation discovery/PINN inversion); OMLT (embed trained ML in Pyomo); Streamlit/Dash + MLflow (delivery). Vendor projection tools (WAVE, IMSDesign-Cloud, Toray tools, LG Q+) are free but non-scriptable — use them only as manual validation oracles.

**Code skeleton to start from:** fork `watertap-org/watertap` flowsheet `RO_with_energy_recovery`; write the NumPy twin of its RO0D equations; wrap fouling ODEs around it; generate 2 synthetic plant-years; then follow Steps 2–5. Every quantitative benchmark you need to beat is in Section 7: soft-sensor ρ ≥ 0.96 / RMSE ≤ 0.023 (Karimanzira 2021), LSTM-MPC tracking ≈ 98.7% (Karimanzira 2020), UF RUL MAE ≤ 4.5 cycles (Khaled 2026), SEC floor 1.06 kWh/m³ and realistic plant range 2–4 kWh/m³ (Elimelech & Phillip 2011), and LCOW arithmetic per WaterTAP's default economics (CRF 9.93%, $0.07/kWh, $30/m², 20%/yr replacement).
