# Primary-Source Verification of Headline Savings Claims in ML-Driven UF/RO Operation

**Purpose.** The §1.1 calibration table carries four families of quantitative claims that §10 flags as untraceable: (a) the Shim et al. CIP-scheduling row (−16.13 % opex, +139.53 % run time), (b) the Veolia Hubgrade fleet claims, (c) the Pani figures underpinning the §6.2 vendor calibration band, and (d) the Gaublomme prediction-horizon discrepancy (2.5 months vs 8 months). This document resolves all four against primary sources, states exactly what each number does and does not license a business case to promise, and closes with what a practitioner needs to reproduce the underlying methods.

**Verification protocol used.** Publisher pages (ScienceDirect, Veolia, ResearchGate) are behind bot protection and returned HTTP 403. Verification therefore proceeded through: (1) Crossref REST API (`api.crossref.org/works?filter=alternative-id:<PII>`) to convert Elsevier PIIs to DOIs and confirm bibliographic identity; (2) OpenAlex (`api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/<DOI>`) for authorship, affiliation, funding and citation counts; (3) full-text green open-access copies in institutional repositories; (4) abstract-carrying aggregators (colab.ws) for closed-access records; (5) vendor primary PDFs fetched from their own CDN origins rather than their CAPTCHA-protected marketing pages. Every claim below is graded **VERIFIED** (traced to an authoritative primary text), **PARTIALLY VERIFIED** (traced to vendor primary material but with no independent audit), or **DEMOTE** (traceable only to secondary/aggregated material, or internally inconsistent).

## 1. Shim et al. 2025 — VERIFIED, but the claim in the table is mis-labelled

### 1.1 Bibliographic identity

The PII `S0011916425006691` resolves via Crossref to **DOI 10.1016/j.desal.2025.119193**, *"Optimizing membrane cleaning strategy of industrial reverse osmosis process using long sequence time-series forecasting"*, Jaegyu Shim, Seunghyeon Lee, Sanghun Park, Jeongwoo Moon, Chulmin Lee, Kyung Hwa Cho, **Desalination vol. 614, article 119193**, issue-dated November 2025, online 15 July 2025 (Crossref, 2025, https://api.crossref.org/works?filter=alternative-id:S0011916425006691). OpenAlex records affiliations at Ulsan National Institute of Science and Technology, Korea University, Pukyong National University and Samsung Electronics, with Chulmin Lee (Samsung Electronics) and Kyung Hwa Cho (Korea University) as corresponding authors; funding from Samsung, Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT, and NRF Korea; 12 citations, top-10 % citation percentile for its year; **closed access, no OA copy exists** (OpenAlex, 2025, https://api.openalex.org/works/https://doi.org/10.1016/j.desal.2025.119193).

### 1.2 The full abstract, recovered verbatim

Retrieved from the CoLab.ws record for the DOI (https://colab.ws/articles/10.1016%2Fj.desal.2025.119193):

> "Membrane cleaning is a crucial maintenance task in filtration processes. However, it is typically guided by manufacturer recommendations or engineers' personal experience without well-defined standards, which can cause inconsistent process control, unnecessary actions, and increased operating costs. Therefore, optimizing operating costs regarding membrane cleaning is essential. In this study, an industrial reverse osmosis (RO) membrane filtration process, which primarily provides treated water for ultrapure water (UPW) production, was investigated to optimize its membrane cleaning strategy. Deep learning-based models were established for long sequence time-series forecasting (LSTF) and applied to investigate the optimal clean-in-place (CIP) scenarios that minimize operating costs while extending the operating time of RO membrane. The attention-based forecasting model showed greater LSTF accuracy (R2=0.82) than a forecasting model combining 1D convolutional neural network and long short-term memory (R2=0.65). Reinforcement learning simulations revealed that adopting a higher pressure threshold for CIP reduces operating costs by considering the high cost of cleaning agents and RO membrane replacement. Furthermore, the modeling process demonstrated that performing CIP twice at the highest pressure threshold in this study would decrease the RO operating cost by 16.13% while increasing RO operating time by 139.53% compared to the previous operation strategy. The findings of this study provide a framework for proactive membrane cleaning, preventing both excessive and delayed cleaning events, and contributing to significant operating cost reductions in filtration processes and UPW production."

Independent corroboration of the opening sentences comes from the NASA ADS record (`2025Desal.61419193S`) and EurekaMag (https://eurekamag.com/research/095/166/095166204.php), both of which reproduce the same lead-in text.

### 1.3 What is confirmed, and what the §1.1 table gets wrong

**Confirmed exactly.** The two-decimal figures are real and appear verbatim in the author-written abstract: **−16.13 % RO operating cost** and **+139.53 % RO operating time**, for the specific scenario of *performing CIP twice at the highest pressure threshold studied*, benchmarked against "the previous operation strategy." Also confirmed: attention-based LSTF **R² = 0.82** versus 1D-CNN+LSTM **R² = 0.65**.

**Three corrections the calibration table needs.**

1. **The process is RO, not UF, and it is an industrial UPW pre-treatment train**, not a municipal or seawater plant. Cost structure is dominated by "the high cost of cleaning agents and RO membrane replacement" — an economics that does not transfer to a low-value municipal UF duty where membrane replacement is amortised differently.

2. **These are simulation outputs, not audited plant results.** The abstract's own framing — "Reinforcement learning simulations revealed…", "the modeling process demonstrated that performing CIP twice … *would* decrease" — is conditional. The number is the objective value of an RL policy evaluated inside a learned forecasting environment, not a measured before/after at the plant. Two decimals are therefore an artefact of floating-point reporting of a simulated scenario comparison, and carry no implied measurement precision.

3. **+139.53 % run time is not a fouling-rate improvement.** Roughly, if CIP is triggered at a *higher* transmembrane-pressure threshold, each run between cleanings is longer by construction. Going from a conservative threshold to the highest threshold with only two CIP events over the horizon more than doubles cumulative operating time (2.3953×) almost definitionally. The genuine scientific claim is that the *cost* of that deferral (faster terminal fouling, higher energy, some irreversible loss of permeability shortening membrane life) is outweighed by avoided chemical and replacement spend — a net −16.13 %. Presenting +139.53 % as a standalone benefit overstates the result; it is the mechanism, not the outcome.

**Recommended table wording:** "−16.1 % simulated RO opex at the cost-optimal CIP pressure threshold (industrial UPW RO, attention-based LSTF surrogate + RL scenario search; Shim et al., Desalination 614:119193, 2025). Simulation only; no audited plant baseline."

### 1.4 The method, reconstructed to implementable depth

The paper is closed access, so the following reconstructs the pipeline from the abstract plus the same group's directly documented prior work (ESEL Lab, Korea University, https://sites.google.com/view/esel20/publications).

**Stage 1 — LSTF surrogate.** "Attention-based forecasting model" in this literature means the Informer family (Zhou et al., AAAI 2021, arXiv:2012.07436, https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07436). Informer's three mechanisms are what make multi-hundred-step membrane-pressure forecasting tractable:

- *ProbSparse self-attention.* Query sparsity is measured by
  $$M(q_i,K)=\ln\sum_{j=1}^{L_K} e^{\frac{q_i k_j^\top}{\sqrt{d}}}-\frac{1}{L_K}\sum_{j=1}^{L_K}\frac{q_i k_j^\top}{\sqrt{d}}$$
  approximated by its max-mean form; only the top $u=c\ln L_Q$ queries participate, giving $O(L\log L)$ time and memory instead of $O(L^2)$.
- *Self-attention distilling*, $X^{t+1}_j=\mathrm{MaxPool}\big(\mathrm{ELU}(\mathrm{Conv1d}([X^t_j]_{AB}))\big)$, halves the sequence between encoder layers.
- *Generative decoder*, input $X_{de}=\mathrm{Concat}(X_{token},X_0)$ with target positions zeroed, emits the whole horizon in one forward pass.

On the ETT benchmarks with horizons {24, 48, 168, 336, 720}, Informer reports MSE reductions of roughly 26.8 % (168-step) to 60.1 % (720-step) versus LSTM baselines. This is the mechanistic reason Shim's attention model reaches R² = 0.82 where a 1D-CNN+LSTM stalls at 0.65: the CNN-LSTM decodes autoregressively and accumulates error over a horizon long enough to reach the next CIP.

**Stage 2 — RL scenario search.** The abstract says "reinforcement learning simulations", and the group's established pattern (Park et al. 2022, below) is: train a deep sequence model as a differentiable plant surrogate, expose it as a Gym-style environment, then let an agent choose cleaning actions. The MDP is naturally:

- State $s_t=[\Delta P_t, Q_{p,t}, T_t, \text{cond}_{f,t}, R_t, \tau_t, n_{CIP,t}]$ — normalised TMP, permeate flow, temperature, feed conductivity, recovery, time since last CIP, cumulative CIP count.
- Action $a_t\in\{\text{continue},\text{CIP}\}$ or, richer, a threshold parameter $\Delta P^{*}$.
- Reward $r_t = -\big(c_E\,\text{SEC}_t Q_{p,t}\Delta t + c_{chem}\mathbb{1}[a_t=\text{CIP}] + c_{mem}\,\delta_{irrev}(a_t) \big) + c_W Q_{p,t}\Delta t$, i.e. energy + chemical + amortised membrane replacement against water produced.

The paper's finding that *higher* thresholds win is a direct consequence of $c_{chem}$ and $c_{mem}$ being large for UPW-grade RO.

## 2. Gaublomme et al. 2023 — horizon discrepancy RESOLVED: both numbers are right, describing different things

PII `S0011916423003880` resolves to **DOI 10.1016/j.desal.2023.116756**, *"A hybrid modelling approach for reverse osmosis processes including fouling"*, Dorien Gaublomme, Ward Quaghebeur, Anse Van Droogenbroeck, Marjolein Vanoppen, Bart De Gusseme, Arne Verliefde, Ingmar Nopens, Elena Torfs, **Desalination 564:116756**, October 2023, online 10 June 2023, 23 citations (Crossref/OpenAlex, 2023). A **full green-OA text is available from Ghent University's repository** (https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/01H8BH8GMBJQ4W31AWX75490QX), which is how the following was verified.

**Resolution of the discrepancy.** Two distinct time windows exist in the paper and the report conflated them:

- **8 months = the test partition of the data-driven fouling model.** "The data was split into a training (2017), validation (01-04/2018) and testing (05-12/2018) dataset in a ratio of around 50/15/35, respectively." May–December 2018 is 8 months.
- **2.5 months = the hybrid-model simulation window.** "The period June 15 to August 31, 2018 was selected to implement the hybrid model since in this period the data-driven RNN-LSTM model has a reasonable prediction for the membrane resistance (Figure 5), which allows to focus on the added value of incorporating accurate fouling behaviour in the model." That is ~2.5 months.

The authors' own general claim is deliberately vague: the RNN-LSTM "is able to accurately predict the general trends in the membrane resistance over a **prediction horizon of several months**." **Neither 2.5 nor 8 months is a validated forecast horizon for the coupled hybrid model.** The honest statement for a planning table: *fouling-trend forecasting demonstrated over an 8-month held-out period; full hybrid mechanistic-plus-fouling simulation demonstrated over a 2.5-month window selected because fouling prediction was good there.* The second window was chosen for favourable conditions, which is a selection caveat worth carrying.

**Plant and dataset (implementable detail).** Full-scale RO at FARYS (Belgian utility) treating Albert Canal surface water; **475,000 m³/year**, each line producing **23–25 m³/h**; four parallel lines, each a double-pass RO followed by electrodeionization; membranes **AK-440 (AK HR Series, SUEZ)**; only the first pass of line 1 was modelled. Raw data: **58 months (August 2015 – end 2019), ~2.56 million 1-minute records**, subsampled to 30-minute steps for modelling.

**Model structure.** Mechanistic solution-diffusion core:
$$J_w = L_p(\Delta P-\Delta \Pi),\qquad J_{s,j}=B_j\,(c_{m,j}-c_{p,j})$$
with concentration polarisation and channel pressure drop. The data-driven part predicts **additional membrane resistance $R_{m,a}$** (units 1/m), which is then fed back into three mechanistic parameters:
$$L_p \leftarrow f(R_m),\qquad h = h_0\left[1+\frac{R_{m,a}}{R_{m,0}}f_h\right],\ f_h=-0.58,\qquad B = B_0\left[1+\frac{R_{m,a}}{R_{m,0}}f_B\right],\ f_B=11.4$$
i.e. fouling simultaneously reduces permeability, constricts the feed-spacer channel (negative $f_h$ → higher pressure drop) and **raises solute passage by an order of magnitude coefficient ($f_B = 11.4$)** — the single most transferable modelling insight in the paper, because it explains why permeate conductivity degrades faster than flux under fouling.

**RNN-LSTM hyperparameters.** One hidden layer of **30 units**, dropout **0.1** between LSTM and dense layer, batch size **32**, **60 epochs**, lookback window of **50 previous steps** (= 25 h at 30-min resolution). Inputs: **feed temperature, feed conductivity, feed calcium concentration, concentrate flow rate, recovery, and CIP occurrence.**

**CIP feature engineering — the paper's most reusable result.** Three encodings of cleaning events were compared: `AfterCleaning` (one-hot post-CIP), `LongCleaning`/`ShortCleaning` (events > 1 day vs ≤ 1 day), and `NumberCleaning` (cumulative count). The LSC variant won: "With the features LongCleaning and ShortCleaning, the prediction is better, showing a clear drop at both CIPs."

**Accuracy.** RMSE on $R_{m,a}$ over the combined validation + test period: **RNN-LSTM with LSC features 7.65**, ARIMAX **13.31**, flux-based mechanistic **14.84**, fourth variant **14.15** (all in 1/m). The learned model roughly halves the error of both classical statistical and purely mechanistic baselines. No R² or NRMSE is reported for downstream variables (feed pressure, permeate conductivity), and no error statistics are given for the 2.5-month hybrid window — a real gap when using this paper as a calibration anchor.

**CIP scheduling is *not* addressed.** The conclusions discuss scenario analysis and real-time operational support but never propose or evaluate CIP timing optimisation. Citing Gaublomme in support of a scheduling business case is a misattribution.

## 3. Veolia Hubgrade — PARTIALLY VERIFIED for named plants; fleet claims are vendor "up-to" marketing; one secondary source must be DEMOTED

Veolia's own domains (`veoliawatertechnologies.com`, `rcp.`, `asia.`, `anz.` subdomains) all return 403/CAPTCHA to automated fetches, including the Nosedo case-study PDFs. The substantive figures were recovered from an independent trade publication that reproduces Veolia's case-study content: *"Hubgrade (2022)"*, Water Projects Online (https://waterprojectsonline.com/innovations/hubgrade-2022/).

**Nosedo WWTP, Milan** — capacity **1,250,000 PE** with full nitrification/denitrification (per the title metadata of Veolia's own case-study PDF at rcp.veoliawatertechnologies.com/…/nosedo_hubgrade_case_study.pdf). Reported outcomes: **€630,000 annual savings**; **energy intensity from 0.431 to 0.323 kWh/kg, a 25 % drop**; hydraulic capacity increased **20–30 % during wet weather**.

**BlueKolding sites, Denmark** — Agtrup WWTP (installed 2007): **−25 % effluent total nitrogen, −45 % chemical precipitant**. After 2011 integration with the sewer network: **+80 % hydraulic load**, extension costs cut **22 % versus original budget**. Sewerflex across the network: hydraulic capacity in storm conditions up **80 %**, sewer overflows down **83 %**, energy consumption down **23 %**, cumulative savings **€2 million**.

**Fleet claims** — across "over 100 wastewater treatment plants worldwide": "up to **30 % of energy for aeration** has been reduced", "**100 % of chemicals** have been cut", "biological capacity has increased by **40 %**", "hydraulic capacity has doubled". The platform exposes "60 standard features."

**Assessment.**

- These are **vendor-authored figures with no independent audit, no stated baseline normalisation, and no confidence intervals.** Each is a best-case from a specific site. The fleet numbers are explicitly "up to" maxima; the "100 % of chemicals have been cut" claim is a single-site ceiling (a plant that eliminated external carbon or precipitant entirely), not a fleet average, and quoting it as a fleet expectation would be indefensible.
- **Critically for a UF/RO report: none of these are membrane figures.** Every verified Hubgrade number is activated-sludge aeration, sewer hydraulics, or chemical precipitation. Aeration energy optimisation (ammonia-based blower control, DO setpoint cascade) is a different physics and a different savings mechanism from membrane fouling management. Hubgrade figures cannot legitimately populate a UF/RO calibration band.
- **DEMOTE the councilofinnovation.com entry.** The claims "€1.5M annual operational savings in case study" and "20 % increase in hydraulic capacity" (https://councilofinnovation.com/impact-stories/ai-optimized-water-treatment-digital-twin/) come from an aggregator that names no plant ("Municipal Water Utility"), cites no external source, self-assigns an "Evidence Score: 5/10", and references a "Blind Verification Protocol with audit ID #PRIV-712" whose report is access-restricted and unverifiable. The €1.5 M figure conflicts with the €630 k traceable to Nosedo. This source should be removed from the report entirely.

## 5. Independent peer-reviewed anchors that *can* carry the calibration band

Because the vendor numbers are unaudited, the band should be triangulated against peer-reviewed work on the same problem class.

- **Park, Shim, Yoon, Lee, Kwak, Lee, Kim, Son & Cho (2022)**, *"Deep reinforcement learning in an ultrafiltration system: Optimizing operating pressure and chemical cleaning conditions"*, **Chemosphere 308:136364** (DOI 10.1016/j.chemosphere.2022.136364, https://colab.ws/articles/10.1016%2Fj.chemosphere.2022.136364). An LSTM surrogate of a UF system provides the DRL environment; the agent controls **three actions: operating pressure, cleaning time, and cleaning concentration.** Results: **specific energy consumption reduced ~20.9 %**, average water flux raised from **39.5 to 43.7 L m⁻² h⁻¹**, operating pressure lowered from **0.617 to 0.540 bar**. This is the closest true UF analogue to the Shim RO result and is the correct anchor for a UF row.

- **Yun, Shim, Moon, Lee, Jeong & Cho (2025)**, *"Towards autonomous operation of two-stage reverse osmosis water treatment system with multi-agent reinforcement learning"*, **Desalination 609:118870** (DOI 10.1016/j.desal.2025.118870, https://colab.ws/articles/10.1016%2Fj.desal.2025.118870). Controls **feed flow rate, high-pressure pump pressure, and inter-stage boosting pump pressure**; compares **Value Decomposition Network (VDN)** and **QMIX** against single-agent **Deep Recurrent Q-Network (DRQN)** over **50,000 training episodes**, minimising SEC subject to production. Both MARL algorithms beat DRQN on performance and robustness. Note the abstract reports no % energy figure — a paper to cite for architecture, not for savings.

- **Moon, Jeong, Chae, Shim, Kim, Cho & Park (2025)**, *"Robust deep learning model combined with missing input data estimation: Application in a 1,000 m³/day high-salinity SWRO plant"*, **Desalination 603:118678** (DOI 10.1016/j.desal.2025.118678). Reported **RMSE 0.375 for TMP and 0.218 for energy consumption (LSTM)**, and **RMSE 0.173 for permeate flow rate (CNN-LSTM)**, explicitly under sensor-dropout conditions. Essential if your plant has gappy historians.

- **Jeong, Son, Yoon, Park, Shim, Kim, Lim & Cho (2021)**, *"Modeling and evaluating performance of full-scale reverse osmosis system in industrial water treatment plant"*, **Desalination 518:115289**. A full-dimension pressure-vessel model of water/salt/foulant transport, validated on **3,875 h** of plant data across **27 operational scenarios**, showing **high dependence of SEC on cleaning frequency within the feed-temperature range** — the mechanistic justification for the whole CIP-optimisation thesis.

- **Ecolab USA (Dörries, Hicks & Krack), US20250296050A1**, *"Membrane monitoring and clean-in-place control"*, filed 21 Mar 2025, published 25 Sep 2025, priority 24 Mar 2024 (https://patents.google.com/patent/US20250296050A1/en). Shows the deployed industrial baseline that ML must beat: fit a first- or higher-order polynomial trend to time-, area- and pressure-normalised flux $F/(t\cdot A\cdot p)$ across production cycles, extrapolate to the predicted capacity-loss date, then schedule deep cleaning at that date **minus a time-safety-margin of 1–6 weeks**; declare a cleaning ineffective when it fails to restore the trend slope by a **threshold of 5–25 %**; establish the trend over **2 weeks to 4 months** post-installation; explicitly exclude CIP and idle phases from trend fitting. No savings figures are quantified.

## Implementation notes

**Data you need, minimum viable.** At least one fouling cycle per CIP regime you intend to compare, and ideally 18–24 months. Gaublomme used 58 months at 1-minute resolution (2.56 M rows) and subsampled to 30 minutes; Jeong et al. validated on 3,875 h. Required tags per RO/UF train: feed pressure, concentrate pressure, permeate pressure, permeate flow, concentrate flow, feed conductivity, permeate conductivity, feed temperature, feed pH, and — non-negotiable — **a timestamped CIP/backwash event log with duration and chemical**. Gaublomme adds feed calcium; for scaling-limited plants add SDI/turbidity and antiscalant dose. Expect to spend most of your effort on the event log: it usually exists only in operator shift notes.

**Normalisation before any model.** Never train on raw pressure. Convert to standard-condition quantities first, using the ASTM/manufacturer-style corrections:
$$\text{NDP}=P_f-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta P_{fc}-P_p-(\pi_f-\pi_p),\qquad A_{norm}=\frac{Q_p\,\text{TCF}}{\text{NDP}\cdot A_m},\qquad \text{TCF}=\exp\!\left[U\left(\tfrac{1}{298}-\tfrac{1}{T+273}\right)\right]$$
with $U\approx 2700$ K for polyamide, and normalised salt passage $SP_{norm}=\frac{c_p}{c_f}\cdot\text{TCF}_{s}$. Model the *normalised* series; otherwise the network spends capacity learning the seasonal temperature cycle. Alternatively follow Gaublomme and model **additional membrane resistance** $R_{m,a}$ from the resistance-in-series form $J_w=\Delta P_{eff}/(\mu(R_m+R_{m,a}))$, which is temperature-corrected by construction through $\mu(T)$.

**Feature engineering for cleaning events.** Implement all three Gaublomme encodings and select empirically: `AfterCleaning` (binary post-event), `ShortCleaning`/`LongCleaning` (split at 1 day of downtime), `NumberCleaning` (cumulative count as an irreversible-fouling proxy). LSC won there and is a sensible default. Add time-since-last-CIP and cumulative throughput since CIP — both are direct MDP state variables later.

**Forecasting model.** Start with the Gaublomme configuration as a baseline because it is fully specified and cheap: single LSTM layer, 30 hidden units, dropout 0.1, lookback 50 steps, batch 32, 60 epochs, ~25 h context at 30-min sampling. Split chronologically, never randomly — 50/15/35 train/val/test by date. Benchmark against ARIMAX; if your LSTM does not beat ARIMAX by a wide margin (7.65 vs 13.31 RMSE in Gaublomme, a 43 % reduction), your features are wrong. Then move to an attention model for long horizons: Informer (`zhouhaoyi/Informer2020`), or the stronger modern baselines PatchTST and DLinear, which frequently match or beat Informer on long-horizon multivariate series. Shim's R² = 0.82 vs 0.65 gap is the expected magnitude of the attention advantage at CIP-interval horizons.

**Hybrid coupling, if you want physics.** Wrap the learned $R_{m,a}$ back into solution-diffusion: $J_w=L_p(\Delta P-\Delta\Pi)$, $J_{s,j}=B_j(c_{m,j}-c_{p,j})$, with $L_p$ degraded by $R_{m,a}$, spacer height $h=h_0[1+(R_{m,a}/R_{m,0})f_h]$ and solute permeability $B=B_0[1+(R_{m,a}/R_{m,0})f_B]$. Calibrate $f_h$ and $f_B$ on held-out data; Gaublomme obtained $f_h=-0.58$, $f_B=11.4$. Do not assume those transfer — but do assume the *sign and asymmetry* transfer: fouling hurts salt rejection far more than it hurts flux.

**Optimisation layer.** Two viable routes.

*Route A — threshold scenario sweep (what Shim actually did, and what you should do first).* Enumerate CIP trigger thresholds $\Delta P^*$ over a grid, roll the forecaster forward under each, count CIP events, and evaluate a cost model:
$$C_{total}=\underbrace{c_E\sum_t \text{SEC}_t Q_{p,t}\Delta t}_{\text{energy}}+\underbrace{n_{CIP}\,c_{CIP}}_{\text{chemicals, labour, downtime}}+\underbrace{\frac{c_{mem}}{L(\{a\})}}_{\text{amortised replacement}}+c_{lost}\!\!\sum_{t\in \text{CIP}}\!\!Q_{p}\Delta t$$
where $L(\{a\})$ is membrane life as a function of the cleaning policy. This is cheap, auditable and directly produces the "%opex vs threshold" curve. Get $c_{CIP}$ and $c_{mem}$ from the site's actual purchase records — the Shim conclusion (higher thresholds win) is entirely driven by their ratio and will invert at a plant with cheap chemicals and expensive energy.

*Route B — RL.* Wrap the surrogate in a Gymnasium environment; use DQN or Deep Recurrent Q-Network for discrete cleaning decisions (partial observability makes DRQN the safer single-agent choice), or VDN/QMIX if you are jointly controlling multiple pumps/stages as in Yun et al. Budget ~50,000 episodes. For continuous pressure/flow setpoints, PPO or SAC. Always evaluate the learned policy against the Route A threshold sweep and against the Ecolab-style polynomial-trend-plus-safety-margin heuristic; RL that cannot beat a linear trend extrapolation with a 1–6 week margin is not deployable.

**Tooling.** Python 3.11+, PyTorch 2.x, `gymnasium`, `stable-baselines3` (DQN/PPO/SAC) or `pymarl2`/`epymarl` (VDN/QMIX), `darts` or `neuralforecast` for Informer/PatchTST/DLinear baselines, `statsmodels` for ARIMAX, `pandas`+`pyarrow` for the historian pull. For the mechanistic side, WAVE (DuPont) or IMSDesign (Hydranautics) will give you $L_p$, $B$ and element geometry for your specific membrane; `WaterTAP` (NAWI, open source, IDAES-based) gives a scriptable RO flowsheet with costing you can couple to a learned fouling term.

**Validation discipline — the whole point of this document.** Report a chronological hold-out, not a random split. Report the naive baselines (persistence, linear trend, ARIMAX) alongside the model. When you report a savings percentage, state the baseline policy explicitly, state whether it is simulated or measured, and never quote a gross saving without the offsetting cost increase — the Pani $700 k/$260 k discrepancy is exactly the failure mode to avoid. If a number has two decimal places, it came from a simulation; round it before it enters a business case.
