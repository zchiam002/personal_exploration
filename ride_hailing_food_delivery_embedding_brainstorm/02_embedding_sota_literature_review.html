1  Executive Summary

This report surveys how state-of-the-art embeddings are generated and — just as importantly — how researchers and industry ensure the maximum benefit is actually harvested from them downstream. It was compiled for the market-state embedding programme (replacing hand-engineered features for ride-hailing and food delivery with a learned per-zone, per-time representation consumed by pricing, dispatch, ETA, repositioning, incentive and forecasting systems). Eleven research areas were surveyed with live web verification; every cited source carries the URL at which it was verified. Ten findings cut across all areas:

  1. The field has converged on “predict in latent space + explicit anti-collapse regularization” as the way to generate embeddings. Reconstruction has been deprecated as the primary objective in every surveyed community — vision SSL (MAE’s weak linear probes vs. the JEPA line), time series (forecasting-as-pretraining), RL (self-predictive ZP losses replacing pixel decoders), and spatiotemporal modeling (masked latent modeling). The programme’s founding skepticism about plain encoder–decoders is independently confirmed by every literature at once. The 2025 frontier (LeJEPA) even derives the isotropic-Gaussian embedding distribution as provably optimal for the linear-probe consumption model this project has committed to.
  2. Auxiliary heads are validated as the central latent-space organizer — and head selection is now a measured problem, not a whiteboard debate. Theory (Maurer 2016; Tripuraneni 2020; JEPA-auxiliary theory 2025) says benefits require the head set to span the function space of downstream tasks (“task diversity”), while near-duplicate heads buy provably little. Practice offers cheap instruments: gradient task-affinity (TAG), linearized subset scoring over hundreds of candidate heads at ~3% of training cost (Grad-TAG), and dense banks of auto-generated heads (Proto-Value Networks). The programme’s “representative heads” question has an operational answer: enumerate candidates, measure affinity in one instrumented run, select the smallest subset covering a basket of downstream probes under a diversity constraint.
  3. Linear probes are simultaneously the field’s constraint, contract, and quality metric. Frozen-encoder linear probing is the standard evaluation everywhere from tabular foundation models to urban region embeddings; industry goes further and treats “a consumer needs a deep model to extract signal” as a defect of the embedding. For versioned contracts, MDL/description-length probing is more stable than raw probe accuracy, and data-efficiency curves (probe skill at 1%/10% of labels) predict how the embedding serves new consumers.
  4. Action-conditioning is non-negotiable — and it is this project’s genuine novelty. No public time-series, tabular, or urban foundation model conditions on control actions; the machinery must be imported from RL and causal inference: action-conditioned latent transition losses (SPR / TD-MPC2 / BYOL-AC), propensity heads that preserve rather than delete confounding information (DragonNet — do not put CFR-style balancing penalties on a shared embedding), and varying-coefficient dose-response heads for the continuous surge lever (VCNet). This corroborates the companion levers/endogeneity analysis (doc 01) from an independent direction.
  5. Every invariance is deleted information. Augmentation and normalization choices are the true prior: instance normalization — universal in time-series foundation models — strips the absolute demand level that pricing needs, so removed statistics must be re-injected; amplitude augmentations would erase weather extremes. Each candidate invariance must be validated against the downstream probe suite before adoption, because the deletion is permanent.
  6. One multi-task embedding serving many consumers is the shipped industrial standard. Pinterest (ItemSage, OmniSearchSage), Netflix’s personalization foundation model, and Airbnb all converged on a single multi-task embedding over per-team embeddings — better metrics, lower maintenance — distributed as a feature-store artifact with declared refresh tiers, backward-compatible version governance (BCT / stationary target geometries), Matryoshka-style nested dimensions, and per-consumer negative-flip release gates.
  7. Spatial machinery must earn its complexity. Learned adaptive adjacency beats hand-specified geographic graphs (Graph WaveNet lesson), travel-time and flow-based edges beat geodesic hex adjacency — but STID showed that per-hex and per-time-slot identity embeddings attached to an MLP capture most of the value. The one-week STID ablation is the cheapest de-risking experiment available before committing to a GNN backbone.
  8. Evaluate against the near future, and prefer boring retraining. Wild-Time shows a ~20% performance cliff between random and temporally-forward splits — forward splits must be the release contract. Across large-scale studies, no temporal-domain-generalization method consistently beats frequent warm-started retraining (with shrink-and-perturb and replay of tail regimes); the “future-decay curve” of frozen probes sets each city’s retraining cadence.
  9. The experimentation substrate is part of the representation system. Marketplace interference (surge in one hex moves drivers and riders in neighbors) breaks unit-level A/B testing and every SUTVA-based causal tool. Clustered switchback designs, always-on lever dithering with logged propensities, and interference-aware estimators (Differences-in-Q’s, Horvitz–Thompson, VCDP) are the upstream preconditions for any causal head — and for honestly measuring the embedding’s own business lift. The causal content of the embedding is bounded by the randomization present in the logs.
  10. The time bucket itself is a hand-engineered feature. The raw data is an asynchronous event stream; neural temporal point process intensity heads with censored likelihoods recover latent demand suppressed under surge — the principled fix for the “realized demand understates true demand” problem — and JODIE-style projection makes embeddings queryable at any timestamp. Verdict from the literature: build event-level machinery as training signal and input pathway, not as the serving backbone.

An honest caveat the literature insists on: deep embeddings do not automatically beat strong feature engineering + gradient-boosted trees on tabular data, and complex spatiotemporal models often lose to embarrassingly simple baselines. Every area recommends benchmarking against the incumbent (hand-engineered features, GBDT, STID, persistence forecasts, latest warm-started checkpoint) before believing any lift — and reporting the concatenation condition (features + embedding) to measure what the embedding actually adds.

2  Scope & Method

Eight parallel research agents each surveyed one area of the literature with live web search (August 2026), under instructions to verify every citation against an arXiv/publisher page or an authoritative index before including it, and to cover seminal foundations, the 2024–2026 state of the art, and industrially deployed methods. An adversarial completeness critic then audited the combined coverage against the project’s requirements and identified three genuine gaps — causal inference under marketplace interference, temporal domain generalization / continual encoder adaptation, and continuous-time event-stream encoding — each of which was closed by a dedicated gap-fill survey. The result is 212 annotated sources across eleven chapters (197 unique after cross-area de-duplication). Each chapter contains: a narrative state-of-the-art summary, the cross-cutting principles of that area, concrete recommendations mapped to the market-state embedding project, and an annotated bibliography. A de-duplicated master index closes the report.

Verification caveat: URLs were checked live at compilation time by the research agents; before quoting any specific claim externally, spot-check the source. Industrial engineering-blog entries document deployed practice but are not peer-reviewed.

3  Coverage Audit (Adversarial Completeness Critique)

The eight areas give strong, largely non-overlapping coverage of encoder objectives (SSL, masked/latent prediction, contrastive), heterogeneous input encoding (tabular, time-series, spatiotemporal-graph), latent-space organization via auxiliary heads, individual-level action-conditioned/causal representation, industrial serving patterns, and evaluation/versioning. Three genuine seams remain, all sitting exactly where the project's hardest requirements live. (1) Most consequential: every causal tool surveyed (TARNet/DragonNet/VCNet, OPE, switch-level self-prediction) assumes SUTVA — no survey covers causal inference and experiment design under cross-unit interference, even though surge in one hex moves drivers and riders in neighboring hexes and the whole marketplace-experimentation literature (switchbacks, two-sided-platform bias, mean-field equilibrium designs, spatial designs — several built on ride-hailing simulators, ICML 2025) exists precisely for this; without it, both the causal auxiliary heads and every embedding-version A/B will be biased. (2) The evaluation-versioning area covers drift DETECTION and version COMPATIBILITY but no survey covers drift ADAPTATION — temporal domain generalization and continual encoder updating (Wild-Time, DRAIN and successors), which governs retraining cadence and forward-generalization of a continuously retrained market-state encoder. (3) All eight surveys silently assume the input is already a per-zone-per-time-bucket tensor; the bucketing itself is a hand-engineered feature the project claims to eliminate. The continuous-time event-stream literature (JODIE/TGN dynamic graph embeddings, neural temporal point processes) encodes raw trip/request events directly, yields embeddings queryable at arbitrary timestamps, and handles demand censoring — an entire missed subfield. Items checked and judged NOT top-3 gaps: discrete/VQ latent tokenization, hierarchical forecast reconciliation, embedding privacy/inversion, LLM event-signal encoding — real but second-order relative to the above.

Gap 1: Causal inference and experiment design under marketplace interference (spatial spillovers, equilibrium effects, switchbacks). Surge price/radius in one zone shifts driver supply and rider demand in neighboring zones and over time, violating SUTVA. The control-causal survey's machinery (TARNet, DragonNet, VCNet, standard IPS/DR OPE) and the evaluation survey's A/B-gated promotion process are all unit-level i.i.d. tools; applied naively they give biased estimates of both the surge dose-response the causal heads are supposed to encode and the business lift of any new embedding version. This literature also prescribes WHAT randomization/logging (switchback schedules, cluster/spatial designs, lever dithering) the platform must run so that action-conditioned representations are identifiable at all — an upstream data-collection requirement no survey states. (closed by its own chapter below)

Gap 2: Temporal distribution shift: temporal domain generalization and continual adaptation of the encoder (not just drift detection). Market data is relentlessly non-stationary — seasonality, city growth, competitor moves, and the platform's own policy changes — and the encoder will be retrained on a cadence forever. The evaluation-versioning survey covers detecting drift and keeping versions compatible, and the control-causal survey covers policy-induced (performative) shift, but no survey covers the methods for making the encoder itself robust to, or adaptive under, gradual temporal drift: how to train so the embedding generalizes to the NEAR FUTURE (the only distribution that matters at serving time), when to warm-start vs fully retrain, and how to evaluate with temporally forward splits. Wild-Time shows existing methods lose ~20% from in-distribution to future-time data — a directly project-relevant failure mode nobody surveyed. (closed by its own chapter below)

Gap 3: Encoding raw event streams: continuous-time dynamic graph embeddings and neural temporal point processes. All eight surveys implicitly accept the per-zone-per-time-bucket aggregation as given — but the bucket width and zone partition are themselves hand-engineered features, the very thing the project set out to eliminate. The underlying data is an asynchronous marked event stream (ride requests, driver session starts/stops, cancellations, deliveries) on a spatial graph. The continuous-time literature — JODIE/TGN-style dynamic graph embeddings and neural temporal point processes — encodes such streams directly, yields embeddings queryable at ANY timestamp (JODIE's projection operator solves the staleness-between-refresh-ticks serving problem), and TPP intensity modeling is the principled way to represent demand under censoring (requests suppressed or abandoned when surge is high — a bias every bucketed count inherits and no survey mentions). (closed by its own chapter below)

4  Self-Supervised Representation Learning Foundations

State of the art

Self-supervised representation learning (SSL) evolved through five paradigm families, each solving the same two-part problem — make embeddings invariant to nuisance variation while preventing collapse to trivial solutions — with different machinery. Contrastive methods (CPC 2018 introduced InfoNCE; SimCLR and MoCo scaled it in 2020) pull augmented views together and push negatives apart. Non-contrastive self-distillation (BYOL, SimSiam, DINO, 2020-21) showed negatives are unnecessary: an asymmetric predictor plus stop-gradient/momentum-teacher suffices. Redundancy-reduction methods (Barlow Twins, VICReg, 2021-22) replaced those architectural tricks with explicit statistical regularization of the embedding covariance — decorrelate dimensions, keep per-dimension variance up. Masked modeling, descended from BERT (Devlin et al. 2018, arXiv:1810.04805), reached vision with MAE (2022) and became modality-general with data2vec, which crucially predicts latent teacher representations rather than raw inputs. That idea — predict in representation space, not input space, so capacity is not wasted modeling unpredictable low-level detail — is the core of LeCun's 2022 position paper "A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (OpenReview, verified via https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/775f42ed458b8c5b0f2094ea4ff5b64c557b1a34) and the JEPA line: I-JEPA (2023), V-JEPA 2 (June 2025, which adds an action-conditioned predictor stage for planning), and LeJEPA (Nov 2025), which derives the isotropic Gaussian as the downstream-risk-optimal embedding distribution and replaces all heuristics with a single distribution-matching regularizer. Current vision SOTA is DINOv3 (Aug 2025, 7B params, Gram anchoring), the first SSL model to beat weakly-supervised counterparts broadly — but its recipe is an engineering-heavy fusion of DINO self-distillation + iBOT masked-latent prediction + Koleo/Gram regularization, reinforcing that the families have converged toward "latent prediction + explicit spectral/distributional regularization." Transfer theory explains why these embeddings work downstream: alignment/uniformity (Wang & Isola 2020) and the dimensional-collapse literature (Jing et al. 2022; 2024-25 cures include orthogonality regularization, LDReg, WERank) show that linear-probe utility tracks how uniformly information is spread across embedding dimensions — effective rank is the key health metric. For heterogeneous non-vision data there is no single winning paradigm (survey: Wang et al. 2024, arXiv:2402.01204): masked/predictive objectives tend to win for forecasting- and imputation-shaped downstream tasks (TimeMAE, MOMENT), contrastive wins for instance discrimination, and tabular practice is converging on hybrid masked+contrastive objectives with domain-agnostic corruptions (SCARF); the JEPA latent-prediction principle is the most natural fit for noisy spatiotemporal state data because it never asks the encoder to reconstruct raw stochastic demand. Open problems: augmentation design outside vision, training stability without heuristics (LeJEPA's target), and evaluation beyond linear probes.

Key principles

  • Every successful SSL method = an invariance/prediction term + an explicit anti-collapse mechanism (negatives, stop-gradient + predictor, teacher centering, variance/covariance regularization, or distribution matching). Choose the mechanism you can measure and monitor in production; the trend from 2020 to 2026 (SimCLR -> VICReg -> LeJEPA) is from implicit architectural tricks toward explicit, provable regularizers.
  • Predict in latent space, not input space (LeCun's core argument, validated by data2vec, I-JEPA, V-JEPA 2): reconstruction objectives spend capacity modeling irreducible input noise and organize the latent space around appearance, not semantics — MAE's weak linear-probe results versus its fine-tuning results are the canonical evidence. For noisy market telemetry, latent prediction keeps only the forecastable structure.
  • Views/augmentations are the prior: whatever you make the encoder invariant to is information permanently deleted from the embedding. In vision this is a settled recipe; for heterogeneous tabular/spatiotemporal data it is the main design decision (SCARF marginal-resampling corruption, temporal shifts, spatial-neighbor masking) and each candidate invariance must be validated against downstream probes before adoption.
  • Downstream (linear) usefulness is governed by how information is spread across dimensions: alignment + uniformity (Wang & Isola) and high effective rank (Jing et al.) predict transfer; dimensional collapse silently destroys tasks that need the discarded dimensions. Embedding covariance eigenspectrum, effective rank, alignment, and uniformity should be first-class release metrics for every embedding version.
  • The projection/predictor head is a buffer that absorbs objective-specific invariance — always publish the pre-projector representation and discard heads (SimCLR finding, reconfirmed across BYOL/VICReg/DINO).
  • Temporal predictive objectives (CPC, JEPA over time) produce 'state-like' embeddings: forcing the future to be predictable from the embedding is the self-supervised analog of decision relevance for forecasting, pricing, and dispatch consumers.
  • Frozen-encoder evaluation (linear probe + k-NN probe) is the field's standard contract and matches the multi-consumer deployment model: one frozen backbone, many cheap heads (DINOv2/v3 practice). Fine-tuning per consumer breaks embedding compatibility and forfeits the shared-representation benefit.
  • Scale-then-distill is the industrial deployment pattern (DINOv2, DINOv3): train the largest affordable teacher once, distill a family of student encoders for different latency budgets; distillation against a fixed teacher also gives a mechanism for cross-version embedding compatibility.
  • Pretraining data curation and balancing matter as much as the objective (DINOv2 LVD-142M, DINOv3): dominant clusters (high-volume zones/hours) must be rebalanced or the embedding under-represents the tail states where decisions matter most.
  • No paradigm universally wins off vision (tabular SSL survey, arXiv:2402.01204; time-series evidence): masked/latent-predictive objectives transfer best to forecasting/imputation-shaped tasks, contrastive to instance-level discrimination, and the tabular consensus is hybrid masked+contrastive with domain-agnostic corruptions — so a market-state encoder should combine a JEPA-style predictive loss with a distributional regularizer rather than bet on one pure family.
  • Heuristic-free stability is a production feature, not an aesthetic one: methods with EMA schedules, warmups, and fragile hyperparameters (classic DINO/BYOL) are risky to retrain continuously; LeJEPA-style single-hyperparameter objectives whose training loss correlates with downstream quality make automated retraining and regression detection tractable.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • Adopt a JEPA-style backbone objective for the market encoder: from a context of observed (zone, time-bucket) cells, predict the latent representations of masked neighboring zones and future time-buckets, conditioned on space-time position (I-JEPA/CPC pattern). Do NOT use raw-value reconstruction as the primary loss — MAE-style objectives demonstrably underperform on the linear-probe interface the project has committed to; keep reconstruction at most as a low-weight auxiliary.
  • Use explicit distributional regularization for anti-collapse instead of negative pairs: VICReg's variance+covariance terms or LeJEPA's SIGReg. Negatives are hazardous here because adjacent zones/time-buckets are false negatives, and the explicit regularizers double as monitorable metrics. LeJEPA is the recommended default: single hyperparameter, no EMA/schedule heuristics, and its isotropic-Gaussian target is provably optimal for exactly the linear-probe downstream contract planned.
  • Design views with SCARF-style random feature corruption (resample a random feature subset from empirical marginals) plus temporal jitter and spatial-neighbor masking; run an ablation matrix confirming each corruption does not degrade any planned downstream probe before locking it in — each invariance deletes information permanently.
  • Handle policy endogeneity with V-JEPA 2's two-stage recipe: (1) pretrain the state encoder action-free (mask/predict objective over logged market data), (2) freeze it and train a separate action-conditioned latent-dynamics predictor that takes surge price/radius as explicit inputs. This keeps control-lever effects out of the state embedding itself while yielding a latent counterfactual simulator ('what does the market state become under surge x') usable by pricing and repositioning.
  • Publish the pre-projector embedding; discard projection/predictor heads at export. Version the encoder, and for v(n+1) use distillation against v(n) outputs on an anchor dataset (DINOv2/v3 pattern) to give consumers approximate backward compatibility; distill one large teacher into small students for latency-constrained consumers (dispatch) vs. larger ones for offline forecasting.
  • Make four embedding-health metrics release gates for every version: covariance eigenspectrum / effective rank (dimensional collapse check, Jing et al.), alignment on known-equivalent market states, uniformity/Gaussianity of the embedding distribution (SIGReg statistic), and a fixed battery of frozen linear + k-NN probes for each downstream task family (surge accuracy, ETA error, demand forecast, imputation of masked zones).
  • Rebalance pretraining data across zones, hours, weather regimes, and event states (DINOv2-style curation): logged data over-represents high-volume downtown weekday states, but decision value concentrates in tail states (storms, events, supply shocks); cluster-and-rebalance before pretraining.
  • Keep the planned auxiliary linear prediction heads, but treat them as probes-with-gradient: small linear heads on the frozen-ish trunk predicting decision-relevant targets (completed trips, wait times, conversion) are consistent with SSL theory (they raise linear accessibility) — while ensuring the SSL predictive loss, not the heads, carries most of the gradient, so the embedding does not collapse to only currently-tracked KPIs.
  • Benchmark honestly against the incumbent: run every downstream probe with (a) hand-engineered features, (b) SSL embedding, (c) concatenation. Tabular SSL literature (survey arXiv:2402.01204) shows deep SSL does not automatically beat strong feature engineering + GBDTs; the concatenation condition reveals what the embedding adds, and hybrid predictive+contrastive objectives are the current tabular consensus if the pure JEPA objective underperforms.
  • Expect the temporal-prediction horizon to be a key hyperparameter (CPC finding): predicting multiple future buckets (e.g., 15/30/60 min) forces the embedding to encode slow market dynamics (supply drift, demand waves) rather than transient noise — align the horizon set with the decision cadences of pricing (minutes) and repositioning (tens of minutes).

Annotated bibliography (18 sources)

2018 van den Oord, A., Li, Y., Vinyals, O. (2018). Representation Learning with Contrastive Predictive Coding. arXiv:1807.03748.
arxiv.org/abs/1807.03748

Introduces InfoNCE: an autoregressive model summarizes past observations into a context vector and is trained to distinguish the true future latent from negatives, maximizing a lower bound on mutual information between context and future. The encoder is generic (conv/RNN), applied to audio, images, text, and RL. Prediction happens in latent space over multiple future steps, so the representation captures slowly-varying 'state' rather than local noise.

Relevance to this project: The closest published template for a market-state encoder: embed a zone's recent history such that the future is linearly predictable from it. Predicting multiple time-buckets ahead in latent space directly optimizes the forecasting-relevance the project's downstream heads need.

2020 Chen, T., Kornblith, S., Norouzi, M., Hinton, G. (2020). A Simple Framework for Contrastive Learning of Visual Representations (SimCLR). ICML 2020 / arXiv:2002.05709.
arxiv.org/abs/2002.05709

Two augmented views of each sample are encoded, passed through a small MLP projection head, and trained with NT-Xent (normalized InfoNCE) using other in-batch samples as negatives. Showed that augmentation composition matters more than architecture, and that the projection head absorbs augmentation-invariance so the pre-projector representation transfers better. Requires large batches for enough negatives.

Relevance to this project: Establishes two rules the project should adopt regardless of paradigm: view/augmentation design is the real prior you impose, and the published embedding should be the pre-projector representation, not the head output.

2020 He, K., Fan, H., Wu, Y., Xie, S., Girshick, R. (2020). Momentum Contrast for Unsupervised Visual Representation Learning (MoCo). CVPR 2020 / arXiv:1911.05722.
arxiv.org/abs/1911.05722

Frames contrastive learning as dictionary lookup: a queue of past embeddings serves as negatives, produced by a momentum-averaged (EMA) copy of the encoder so keys stay consistent as the encoder evolves. Decouples negative count from batch size, making contrastive training feasible at modest compute.

Relevance to this project: The EMA-teacher trick recurs in BYOL/DINO/data2vec and is the standard way to get stable targets during training — useful for a market encoder retrained continuously on streaming data. The queue idea also suggests how to hold negatives across time-buckets without giant batches.

2020 Wang, T., Isola, P. (2020). Understanding Contrastive Representation Learning through Alignment and Uniformity on the Hypersphere. ICML 2020 / arXiv:2005.10242.
arxiv.org/abs/2005.10242

Proves that the InfoNCE loss asymptotically decomposes into alignment (positive pairs map close) and uniformity (features spread uniformly on the hypersphere), and that directly optimizing these two measurable quantities matches contrastive performance. Both metrics are cheap to compute on any embedding batch.

Relevance to this project: Gives the project two concrete, model-agnostic health metrics for every embedding version: alignment against known-equivalent market states and uniformity of the embedding distribution. Uniformity is essentially why linear probes work — information is spread instead of clumped.

2020 Grill, J.-B., Strub, F., Altché, F., et al. (2020). Bootstrap Your Own Latent: A New Approach to Self-Supervised Learning (BYOL). NeurIPS 2020 / arXiv:2006.07733.
arxiv.org/abs/2006.07733

An online network predicts the EMA target network's embedding of a different augmented view of the same input; no negatives at all. Collapse is avoided by the asymmetry: a predictor MLP on the online branch plus the slow-moving target. Matched contrastive SOTA and proved negatives are not fundamental.

Relevance to this project: Removes the biggest contrastive headache for market data: defining negatives when nearby zones/adjacent time-buckets are genuinely similar (false negatives). A BYOL-style setup sidesteps that entirely.

2021 Chen, X., He, K. (2021). Exploring Simple Siamese Representation Learning (SimSiam). CVPR 2021 / arXiv:2011.10566.
arxiv.org/abs/2011.10566

Strips BYOL to its minimum: a shared Siamese encoder, a predictor on one branch, and a stop-gradient on the other — no momentum encoder, no negatives, no large batches. Shows stop-gradient is the essential ingredient preventing collapse, with the predictor implicitly implementing an EM-like alternating optimization.

Relevance to this project: The minimal, cheapest-to-train non-contrastive baseline — a good first ablation for the market encoder before adding heavier machinery. Its lesson: the anti-collapse mechanism, not scale, is what you must get right.

2021 Caron, M., Touvron, H., Misra, I., Jégou, H., Mairal, J., Bojanowski, P., Joulin, A. (2021). Emerging Properties in Self-Supervised Vision Transformers (DINO). ICCV 2021 / arXiv:2104.14294.
arxiv.org/abs/2104.14294

Self-distillation with no labels: a student ViT matches the EMA teacher's softmax output distribution over multi-crop views, with centering and sharpening of teacher outputs preventing collapse. Discovered that SSL ViT features contain emergent semantic structure (segmentation-quality attention maps) and excel at k-NN retrieval without any fine-tuning.

Relevance to this project: Demonstrates that a well-trained SSL embedding supports nonparametric (k-NN) consumption — relevant for 'find historical market states similar to now' use-cases in dispatch/incentive tooling. Multi-crop (global vs. local views) maps naturally to city-wide vs. zone-local market context.

2021 Zbontar, J., Jing, L., Misra, I., LeCun, Y., Deny, S. (2021). Barlow Twins: Self-Supervised Learning via Redundancy Reduction. ICML 2021 / arXiv:2103.03230.
arxiv.org/abs/2103.03230

Computes the cross-correlation matrix between embeddings of two distorted views across the batch and drives it toward identity: diagonal terms enforce invariance, off-diagonal terms decorrelate dimensions so no information is redundantly encoded. Needs no negatives, stop-gradients, momentum encoders, or large batches.

Relevance to this project: Decorrelated dimensions are exactly what makes linear probes and downstream linear heads efficient — each embedding coordinate carries distinct information. A natural objective when the project wants a compact, non-redundant market-state vector consumed by many teams.

2022 Bardes, A., Ponce, J., LeCun, Y. (2022). VICReg: Variance-Invariance-Covariance Regularization for Self-Supervised Learning. ICLR 2022 / arXiv:2105.04906.
arxiv.org/abs/2105.04906

Three explicit loss terms: invariance (MSE between views), variance (hinge keeping each embedding dimension's std above a threshold — directly preventing collapse), and covariance (pushing off-diagonal covariance to zero — preventing dimensional collapse). Because the branches need not share weights or architecture, it extends to multimodal/heterogeneous inputs.

Relevance to this project: The most transferable anti-collapse recipe for non-vision data: no augmentation-dependent negatives, and the two branches can be different encoders (e.g., demand-side vs. supply-side towers of the market encoder). Its variance/covariance terms double as monitorable production metrics.

2022 Jing, L., Vincent, P., LeCun, Y., Tian, Y. (2022). Understanding Dimensional Collapse in Contrastive Self-supervised Learning. ICLR 2022 / arXiv:2110.09348.
arxiv.org/abs/2110.09348

Shows that even contrastive methods with negatives suffer dimensional collapse — the embedding covariance spectrum decays so representations occupy a low-rank subspace — driven by strong augmentation and implicit weight-matrix rank regularization. Proposes DirectCLR, which optimizes only a sub-vector of the representation directly, outperforming SimCLR with a trainable projector.

Relevance to this project: Defines the failure mode most dangerous to this project: an embedding that looks fine on one downstream task but has silently discarded the dimensions other tasks need. Mandates tracking the embedding eigenspectrum/effective rank as a release gate for every embedding version.

2022 He, K., Chen, X., Xie, S., Li, Y., Dollár, P., Girshick, R. (2022). Masked Autoencoders Are Scalable Vision Learners (MAE). CVPR 2022 / arXiv:2111.06377.
arxiv.org/abs/2111.06377

Masks 75% of image patches; an encoder sees only visible patches and a lightweight decoder reconstructs the masked pixels, making pretraining 3x+ faster. The asymmetric design keeps the encoder free of mask tokens. Fine-tuning results are excellent, but linear-probe results lag joint-embedding methods — reconstruction targets retain low-level information not organized for linear separability.

Relevance to this project: A cautionary result for the project's plan: pure reconstruction (autoencoding) objectives underperform on linear probes, precisely the consumption interface the project has committed to. Masking is still valuable — as the corruption strategy, not as a pixel/raw-value reconstruction target.

2022 Baevski, A., Hsu, W.-N., Xu, Q., Babu, A., Gu, J., Auli, M. (2022). data2vec: A General Framework for Self-supervised Learning in Speech, Vision and Language. ICML 2022 / arXiv:2202.03555.
arxiv.org/abs/2202.03555

One recipe across three modalities: a student sees a masked input and regresses the EMA teacher's contextualized latent representations (averaged over top-K layers) of the full input. 5  Time-Series Representation Learning & Foundation Models

State of the art

Time-series representation learning has moved through three distinct waves. (1) 2021-2023: contrastive/self-supervised methods (TNC, TS-TCC, TS2Vec, CoST) learned embeddings from unlabeled series by defining what should be invariant (augmented views, temporal neighborhoods) and what should be discriminated, evaluated almost universally by LINEAR PROBES on frozen embeddings — TS2Vec's hierarchical timestamp-to-series representations and CoST's causally-motivated seasonal-trend disentanglement are the high-water marks; SimMTM then showed masked reconstruction beats contrast when the reconstruction task is softened (neighbor-aggregated recovery). (2) 2023: PatchTST established patching (sub-series tokens) + instance normalization as the canonical tokenization for transformers, with channel-independence as a robustness trade-off. (3) 2023-2026: the foundation-model wave — Chronos (values-as-language tokens), TimesFM (decoder-only next-patch prediction), Moirai (any-variate, any-frequency masked encoder trained on the 27B-observation LOTSA corpus), MOMENT (embedding-oriented, multi-task), Lag-Llama (lags+calendar covariates), then scaling/specialization via sparse MoE (Time-MoE at 2.4B params on 300B points; Moirai-MoE with token-level expert specialization), and generative/continuous heads (Sundial's flow-matching TimeFlow loss on a 1T-point corpus, ICML 2025 Oral, arXiv:2502.00816; Moirai 2.0's decoder-only multi-quantile simplification, arXiv:2511.11698). The 2025-2026 SOTA frontier on the standard zero-shot benchmarks GIFT-Eval (arXiv:2410.10393) and fev-bench is held by covariate-capable, in-context-learning models: Chronos-2 (group attention over targets+covariates), TabPFN-TS (tabular prior-fitted network with calendar features), TiRex (35M-param xLSTM, NeurIPS 2025, arXiv:2505.23719) and TiRex-2 (multivariate + streaming with constant per-patch cost, July 2026); industrial deployments include Datadog's Toto trained on its own observability telemetry with the BOOM benchmark (arXiv:2505.14766) and TimesFM 2.5 in production at Google (16k context, continuous quantile head). Open problems directly relevant here: exogenous/known-future covariate handling only became first-class in 2025 (Chronos-2, TabPFN-TS, TiRex-2 — before that, weather-like inputs were ignored by most TSFMs); forecasting skill does not equal embedding quality (MOMENT is the main embedding-first TSFM; 2026 probing work shows intermediate layers beat final layers and that non-stationarity is only partially linearly accessible in TSFM embeddings, with model-specific failure modes — Choi et al. 2026); multivariate dependency modeling vs channel independence remains contested; and none of the public models condition on control actions, so policy endogeneity must be handled by the practitioner via the covariate channel.

Key principles

  • Linear probing on frozen embeddings is THE standard protocol of this literature (TS2Vec, TNC, TS-TCC, MOMENT, Mantis all evaluate this way) — the project's plan to force decision-relevant information to be linearly accessible via linear-probe auxiliary heads is exactly aligned with how the field defines representation quality.
  • Multi-resolution embeddings maximize downstream reuse: TS2Vec's hierarchical contrast yields representations at timestamp, sub-window, and series level from one encoder; different downstream consumers (dispatch needs per-minute state, forecasting needs per-hour aggregates) can pool the same latent sequence at different granularities.
  • The choice of augmentation/invariance IS the choice of what information the embedding discards: scaling/jitter augmentations (TS-TCC) impose amplitude invariance, and instance normalization (PatchTST, all TSFMs) strips level and scale. For market state, absolute demand level and weather extremes are decision-relevant — invariances must be audited, and normalization statistics must be re-injected as features.
  • Forecasting-as-pretraining (next-patch prediction in TimesFM, Time-MoE, Moirai 2.0) organizes the latent space around predictive sufficiency rather than reconstruction fidelity — closer to decision value than autoencoding. Mixed objectives (masked reconstruction + forecasting + contrastive, as in MOMENT/SimMTM ablations) consistently beat any single objective.
  • Patching is the canonical tokenization: sub-series patches retain local semantics, cut attention cost quadratically, extend usable context, and give per-patch token embeddings that downstream models can attend over rather than a single pooled vector (PatchTST, TimesFM, Moirai).
  • Channel independence is robust but lossy; the 2024-2026 SOTA reintroduces cross-variate information through dedicated mechanisms — variate-level cross-attention (TimeXer), any-variate attention with variate encodings (Moirai), group attention across targets+covariates (Chronos-2), asymmetric variate mixers (TiRex-2). Cross-signal structure (demand-supply interaction, weather-to-demand) must flow through explicit attention paths, not be averaged away.
  • Covariates need a taxonomy: static (zone geometry), past-observed (realized demand, weather), and known-future (weather FORECASTS, holidays, planned events) — introduced by TFT and only adopted by foundation models in 2025 (Chronos-2, TabPFN-TS, TiRex-2). Known-future inputs are the single largest accuracy lever on covariate-rich benchmarks (Chronos-2's biggest wins are on covariate-informed tasks).
  • Multi-horizon probabilistic output heads (TFT's quantile losses, Moirai's mixture distributions, Sundial's flow matching, TimesFM 2.5's continuous quantile head) force the latent to encode uncertainty and horizon-dependent dynamics — downstream pricing/dispatch consume distributions, not point estimates, and quantile heads are cheap auxiliary objectives.
  • Disentanglement along generative factors improves shift robustness: CoST's causal argument is that separating seasonal from trend components yields representations stable under intervention/distribution shift — directly relevant when surge policy (an intervention) changes the data-generating process.
  • Heterogeneity is handled by conditional capacity, not one dense model: frequency-specific projections proved too coarse, and token-level sparse MoE (Moirai-MoE, Time-MoE) lets different dynamics regimes (rush hour vs overnight, rain vs clear) route to different experts automatically.
  • TSFM embeddings are not automatically fit for purpose: 2026 probing shows intermediate-layer representations are more discriminative than final-layer ones, and non-stationarity detectability degrades smoothly with model-specific failure modes (Choi et al. 2026) — embedding quality must be measured per-task, per-layer, not assumed from forecasting leaderboards.
  • Evaluation infrastructure is a first-class artifact: the field converged on standardized, leakage-audited, rolling-origin benchmarks spanning domains/frequencies/horizons (GIFT-Eval, fev-bench, BOOM). A versioned company-wide embedding needs the internal equivalent: a fixed probe-task suite that every embedding version must pass before release.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • Architecture: patch-tokenize each zone's multivariate history (PatchTST) but separate endogenous targets (demand, supply, imbalance) from exogenous context (weather, events, holidays) TimeXer-style — patch-level self-attention for targets, variate-level tokens + cross-attention for covariates — and adopt TFT's covariate taxonomy: static zone features, past-observed signals, and KNOWN-FUTURE inputs (weather forecasts, scheduled events, planned incentive campaigns). Use Moirai-style any-variate attention with variate-ID encodings so new signals can be added across embedding versions without retokenizing the world.
  • Treat logged control actions (surge multiplier, surge radius, dispatch parameters) as first-class input covariates: past actions as past-observed covariates, planned/hypothetical actions as known-future covariates. No public TSFM conditions on actions, so this is where the bespoke encoder earns its keep — an action-conditioned encoder is the minimal structure needed so the latent represents state rather than a state-policy entanglement, and it enables counterfactual queries ('embedding under multiplier 1.0 vs 1.8') that the causal/RL literature area will build on.
  • Objective: use a weighted mixture — (a) multi-horizon multi-quantile forecasting heads for key targets at several horizons (TFT/Moirai-2.0-style; this is simultaneously the auxiliary 'decision-value' objective from the project brief and a probabilistic output downstream pricing needs), (b) SimMTM-style neighbor-aggregated masked-patch reconstruction for completeness of state capture, and (c) TS2Vec-style hierarchical contrast so per-time-bucket latents pool coherently into per-hour/per-day embeddings. Constrain all auxiliary heads to linear/shallow probes on the frozen trunk, as planned — this matches the field's standard representation-quality protocol.
  • Normalization: apply reversible instance normalization per zone-window for training stability (universal in TSFMs), but re-inject the removed statistics (level, scale, recent trend of raw demand/supply) as explicit embedding inputs or side-channel features — absolute demand level is decision-critical for pricing and would otherwise be destroyed. Audit every augmentation for invariances that erase decision signal: no amplitude scaling or jitter on demand magnitudes or weather extremes; prefer TS2Vec-style cropping/masking positives and TNC-style temporal-neighborhood positives (and adjacent-zone positives) instead.
  • Before building anything bespoke, benchmark three zero-shot baselines on the exact downstream probe tasks: (1) frozen MOMENT embeddings + linear probes, (2) Chronos-2 with zone groups + weather covariates for the forecasting tasks, (3) TabPFN-TS over featurized zone-time rows. These set the bar the custom encoder must clear, and Chronos-2/TiRex-2 fine-tuned on company telemetry may be the fastest path to a strong v1 trunk (TimesFM 2.5 demonstrates LoRA-style cheap adaptation for versioned updates).
  • Serving/streaming: for a per-zone per-5-minute embedding computed fleet-wide, prefer either a small recurrent state-passing design (TiRex-2's xLSTM: constant cost per new patch, unbounded context, and the hidden state IS the market state) or sparse MoE capacity (Time-MoE/Moirai-MoE) if transformer-based — token-level expert routing also handles regime heterogeneity (commute vs nightlife vs storm) and yields an interpretable regime label for free.
  • Cross-zone structure: implement spatial context via in-context groups (Chronos-2 group attention over a zone and its neighbors) or any-variate attention over stacked neighbor series, rather than a separate graph model in v1 — the 2025-2026 evidence is that attention over related series in-context captures cross-series dependencies zero-shot, and city-level demand shocks (weather fronts, event endings) propagate across zones.
  • Evaluation suite as a versioned artifact (GIFT-Eval/fev-bench pattern): a fixed internal benchmark of frozen-encoder linear-probe tasks — multi-horizon demand/supply forecasting, ETA regression, anomaly/incident detection, surge-regime classification — with rolling-origin temporal splits, strict leakage audits, and per-LAYER probing (2026 evidence: intermediate layers are often more discriminative than final layers, so consider exposing a designated 'embedding layer' rather than the last one). Add Choi-et-al.-style controlled non-stationarity injections (synthetic mean/variance/trend shifts in demand) to verify regime shifts remain linearly detectable in every release — surge decisions are precisely non-stationarity detection.
  • Cover rare regimes with synthetic data: every SOTA TSFM leans on synthetic pretraining (Chronos KernelSynth, Chronos-2's synthetic multivariate structures, TiRex-2's on-the-fly covariate coupling, Sundial's TimeBench). Generate synthetic extreme-weather, concert-surge, and supply-outage scenarios — including counterfactual action traces — to de-bias the policy-endogenous logged corpus and cover the tail regimes where pricing decisions matter most.
  • Version compatibility: freeze and archive each encoder version; gate releases on the probe benchmark (no probe task may regress beyond tolerance); and when the latent space changes, ship a learned linear adapter mapping v(n) embeddings into v(n+1) space so downstream teams migrate asynchronously — the TSFM ecosystem's practice of maintaining stable model families with quantile-head add-ons and LoRA deltas (TimesFM 1.0→2.5, Chronos→Chronos-2, Moirai→2.0) rather than breaking rewrites is the operational pattern to copy.
  • Output distributions, not points: whatever trunk is chosen, expose multi-horizon quantile (or sampled-trajectory, Sundial-style flow-matching) forecast heads alongside the embedding — surge pricing and repositioning consume uncertainty (P90 imbalance, probability of undersupply), and the field's uniform move to probabilistic heads (TFT quantiles, Moirai mixtures, TimesFM 2.5 continuous quantile head, Chronos-2 quantiles) reflects that point forecasts are insufficient for control decisions.

6  Tabular & Heterogeneous Business Data Embeddings

State of the art

Tabular representation learning evolved in four waves. (1) Entity embeddings of categoricals (Guo & Berkhahn 2016) replaced one-hot encoding with learned Euclidean vectors, becoming standard practice in industrial ML. (2) Transformer-era architectures (TabTransformer 2020, FT-Transformer 2021, SAINT 2021) established the "feature-as-token" paradigm: every column — categorical or numerical — is embedded into a vector and contextualized by attention; Gorishniy et al. (2022) then showed that HOW numerics are embedded (piecewise-linear or periodic encodings rather than raw scalars) is a decisive, architecture-independent performance lever. (3) Tabular self-supervision (VIME 2020, SCARF 2022, SAINT's CutMix/MixUp contrastive pretraining, and later augmentation-free latent-prediction methods like T-JEPA 2024) found that corruption-and-recovery and contrastive objectives beat plain autoencoder reconstruction, though gains on tabular data are more modest and inconsistent than in vision/NLP — which is why auxiliary supervised signal remains important. (4) Tabular foundation models are the current SOTA frontier: cross-table pretraining with shared backbones (XTab 2023), schema-free graph-attention over string-embedded cells (CARTE 2024), LLM-based row serialization (TabuLa-8B 2024), and above all in-context-learning prior-fitted networks — TabPFN v2 (Nature 2025), TabICL (ICML 2025), TabDPT (NeurIPS 2025), TabPFN-2.5 (Nov 2025), and 2026 successors (TabICLv2, LimiX-2M, generalist embedding models like TabEmbed, benchmarked on TabArena). Notably, Ye et al. (2025) showed TabPFN v2 works as a frozen feature extractor whose embeddings are nearly linearly separable — directly validating linear-probe-based embedding design. In parallel, multimodal fusion of tabular + time-series + spatial + weather streams matured from the Temporal Fusion Transformer's typed-covariate gating (2021) to deployed industrial systems: Uber's DeepETA (multi-resolution hashed geospatial embeddings + linear transformer, 2022) and DiDi's MVGR-Net (WWW 2026), which contrastively aligns POI-semantic and mobility views of geo-zones before demand forecasting. Open problems: parity with tuned GBDTs on medium-sized data is still contested (well-tuned MLP ensembles like TabM remain highly competitive), tabular SSL objectives lack a clear winner, context-length/latency limits of ICL models constrain serving, and none of this literature addresses policy endogeneity of logged actions — that must be imported from the causal/RL representation literature.

Key principles

  • Feature-as-token is the unifying interface: embed every input — categorical, numerical, spatial, temporal — into a d-dimensional token and let attention contextualize them (TabTransformer, FT-Transformer, SAINT). This gives heterogeneous business data one uniform representation and a single pooled [CLS]-style vector to publish.
  • Numeric features must be explicitly embedded, not fed as raw scalars: piecewise-linear (quantile or target-aware bins) and periodic embeddings deliver large, architecture-independent gains (Gorishniy et al. 2022) and were independently rediscovered in production at Uber (bucketize-and-embed beat raw continuous inputs).
  • High-cardinality spatial IDs need multi-resolution + hashing: quantize locations at several grid resolutions and map cells to embeddings via multiple feature hashing — better accuracy, less memory, and graceful handling of unseen cells (Uber DeepETA); pure entity embeddings (Guo & Berkhahn) remain right for moderate-cardinality categoricals.
  • Reconstruction alone is the weakest pretraining signal: mask-estimation (VIME), contrastive learning over randomly corrupted feature subsets (SCARF, SAINT), masked-column prediction on real tables (TabDPT), and latent-space prediction (T-JEPA) all organize the latent space better than autoencoding — but tabular SSL gains are smaller and less reliable than in vision/NLP, so auxiliary supervised heads carry real weight.
  • Linear separability is both the mechanism and the metric of downstream usefulness: the best tabular encoders produce nearly linearly separable embedding spaces (Ye et al. on TabPFN v2), and linear probing is the standard way to verify that decision-relevant information is cheaply accessible — validating the project's linear-probe auxiliary-head design.
  • Transfer across heterogeneous tables requires decoupling schema from backbone: either per-table featurizers around a shared trunk (XTab), schema-free string/graph encodings (CARTE), or distribution-aware column embeddings (TabICL). This is what makes one encoder serve many cities, business lines, and evolving schemas.
  • Context conditioning beats static encoding: ICL foundation models make a row's representation depend on a reference set (TabPFN, TabICL, TabDPT), and intersample attention (SAINT) lets rows borrow from similar rows — for market state this means embeddings can be conditioned on the current regime rather than a fixed global mapping.
  • Typed multimodal fusion beats naive concatenation: separate static / observed-past / known-future input pathways with variable-selection gating (TFT), and contrastive alignment of complementary views of the same spatial unit (DiDi MVGR-Net POI + mobility), consistently outperform flat feature stacking.
  • Pretraining data mix matters and scaling laws hold: synthetic causally-structured priors give broad robustness (TabPFN), but real in-domain tables add signal synthetics miss and improve downstream generalization (TabDPT); performance scales as power laws in model and data size.
  • Serve via distillation and frozen encoders: foundation-scale teachers can be distilled into compact MLP/tree students with orders-of-magnitude lower latency (TabPFN-2.5), and frozen encoders with lightweight task heads preserve generalization while keeping the embedding contract stable for many consumers.
  • One embedding space can serve many consumption modes — prediction, retrieval, clustering, similarity search — if trained with multi-task contrastive objectives and hard negatives (TabEmbed 2026), but it must be evaluated on a benchmark covering all intended uses, not just one task's accuracy.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • Front-end: adopt an FT-Transformer-style feature tokenizer as the encoder's input layer — periodic or piecewise-linear embeddings (quantile bins; consider target-aware bins keyed to a core task like conversion) for all numeric streams (demand counts, supply, ETAs, precipitation, temperature); entity embeddings for moderate-cardinality categoricals (time bucket, day type, event category); and multi-resolution geohash grids with multiple feature hashing for zone identity, following Uber DeepETA.
  • Pretraining objective: use a composite of masked-feature modeling with mask estimation (VIME) and SCARF-style contrastive corruption over unlabeled zone-hour rows, NOT a plain autoencoder; weight these below the planned auxiliary supervised heads, since the literature shows tabular SSL alone underdelivers — the linear-probe prediction heads are what will organize the space by decision value.
  • Structure the encoder's temporal fusion like TFT: three typed pathways — static zone attributes, observed-past series (demand, supply, completed trips), and known-future inputs (weather forecasts, scheduled events, holidays) — with variable-selection gating; this also yields per-stream attributions that build trust with downstream teams. Treat the surge price and surge radius levers as explicit action tokens in the observed pathway so the representation is action-conditioned rather than silently policy-confounded (full causal correction must come from the causal-representation workstream).
  • Make linear probing the official evaluation harness: for every downstream task (pricing, dispatch, ETA, repositioning, incentives, forecasting) maintain a frozen-encoder linear probe suite, and gate encoder releases on probe metrics — this mirrors both the project's own auxiliary-head design and the field's standard (Ye et al. 2025 shows the best encoders make tasks linearly solvable).
  • Baseline before building: extract embeddings from TabPFN v2 / TabPFN-2.5 (the API exposes them) and from a tuned GBDT on hand-engineered features, and require the custom encoder to beat both on the linear-probe suite; use TabArena-style rigorous protocols to avoid the field's chronic weak-baseline problem.
  • Pretrain on your own logged tables, not just public/synthetic data: TabDPT shows real in-domain rows materially improve transfer; the company's historical zone-hour logs across all cities are exactly the large real corpus the scaling laws reward.
  • For multi-city and cold-start: share the transformer backbone across cities/business lines with lightweight per-city featurizers (XTab pattern), and consider CARTE-style string-grounded column encoding so new signal feeds and new-market schemas onboard without schema matching or encoder retraining.
  • Add a multi-view zone-pretraining stage as DiDi does in production: contrastively align each zone's static-semantic view (POI composition, road network) with its dynamic-mobility view (trip flows), so the market-state embedding inherits both; inject discrete external events as first-class inputs rather than hoping the encoder infers them.
  • Serving and versioning: keep the published encoder frozen between versions with lightweight adapters/heads per consumer; for request-time latency, distill the full encoder into a compact MLP student (TabPFN-2.5's distillation-engine pattern); on each new version, train with an alignment/distillation loss to the previous embedding geometry and re-run the frozen linear-probe suite of every consumer before rollout to guarantee backward compatibility.
  • Consider context-conditioned inference for regime awareness: an ICL-style pathway (TabICL's row-embedding-then-context architecture) that conditions today's zone-hour embedding on a reference window of recent city-wide data would let the same encoder adapt to demand shocks and seasonal regime shifts without retraining — evaluate it against the static encoder on shock periods (storms, concerts, holidays).

7  Auxiliary Tasks & Multi-Task Learning as Latent-Space Organizers

State of the art

Auxiliary-task and multi-task learning as a way to ORGANIZE a latent space began with UNREAL (Jaderberg et al., 2016), which showed that a few cheap prediction heads sharing an encoder dramatically improve representations when the primary signal is weak — exactly the project's bet. The field then split into four strands that have now converged. (1) THEORY: Maurer et al. (2016) and Tripuraneni et al. (2020) proved that a shared representation trained on T diverse tasks cuts per-task sample complexity, with "task diversity" — the heads must span the function space downstream tasks live in — as the load-bearing condition; Lyle et al. (2021) characterized WHICH auxiliary tasks induce which latent geometry (value-like heads pull representations toward the transition operator's top eigenspace), and 2025 JEPA theory (Yu et al.) proved auxiliary regression heads provably prevent representation collapse in predictive encoders. (2) WHICH TASKS TRAIN TOGETHER: Standley et al. (2020) showed semantic relatedness does not predict training compatibility; TAG (Fifty et al., 2021) reduced grouping to measurable inter-task gradient affinity in one run; Grad-TAG (KDD 2024) scores all task subsets via linearization at ~3% of full-training cost up to 500 tasks; and ICLR 2025 work (Jeong & Yoon) exploits proximal affinity online during training. (3) GRADIENT CONFLICT: uncertainty weighting (Kendall et al., 2018), GradNorm (2018), PCGrad (2020) and CAGrad (2021) remedy scale imbalance and destructive interference, but Kurin et al. (2022) showed well-regularized plain loss summation often matches them — measurement before machinery. (4) WHAT A REPRESENTATION SHOULD RETAIN AND HOW TO CHECK: the information-bottleneck view (Alemi et al., 2017) frames the embedding as a minimal sufficient statistic for the chosen prediction targets; Rakelly et al. (2021) proved popular self-supervised objectives can be provably INSUFFICIENT for control, and Khetarpal et al. (2024/25) showed action-conditional self-prediction (BYOL-AC) recovers per-action distinctions that policy-averaged objectives lose — directly relevant to policy-endogenous surge levers; probing (Alain & Bengio 2016; MDL probing, Voita & Titov 2020) supplies the evaluation contract. The project's key open question — choosing a SMALL set of heads to shape a general-purpose embedding — remains only partially solved: Proto-Value Networks (Farebrother et al., 2023) showed hundreds of auto-generated successor-measure heads yield frozen embeddings that linear heads can decode to near-DQN performance, while automated selection is attacked by meta-gradient discovery of GVF questions (Veeriah et al., 2019, arXiv:1909.04607), bilevel weighting (AuxiLearn, 2021; Auto-Lambda, TMLR 2022, arXiv:2202.03091), evolutionary loss search (A2LS, NeurIPS 2022, arXiv:2210.06041), generate-and-test discovery (Rafiee et al., arXiv:2210.14361), and RL-based task generation (RL-AUX, 2025, arXiv:2510.22940). No a-priori theory yet picks the minimal head set for a given downstream portfolio; the practical SOTA is cheap measured affinity plus diversity-constrained subset selection, validated by probe scorecards.

Key principles

  • Auxiliary heads shape representations through their GRADIENTS, not their outputs: the value of a head is which distinctions it forces the shared encoder to preserve. Recent JEPA theory (Yu et al. 2025) makes this precise — auxiliary heads act as anchors that provably prevent collapse of exactly the distinctions they predict, so heads should be chosen per downstream decision family.
  • Task diversity is the load-bearing theoretical condition (Tripuraneni et al. 2020; Maurer et al. 2016): benefits require the head set to SPAN the function space downstream consumers live in. A small set of near-duplicate targets (e.g., several demand proxies) buys provably little; coverage of distinct decision-relevant function directions matters more than head count.
  • Sufficiency must be defined relative to downstream decisions, and observational prediction is not enough under policy endogeneity: plausible self-supervised/MI objectives can be provably insufficient for control (Rakelly et al. 2021), and policy-averaged forecasting collapses per-action distinctions that action-conditional prediction preserves (Khetarpal et al. 2024/25). When the logged data is generated by control levers, at least some heads must be action-conditioned.
  • Semantic relatedness does not predict training compatibility (Standley et al. 2020); affinity is an empirical, regime-dependent quantity. It is now cheap to measure: inter-task gradient affinity in one instrumented run (TAG 2021), linearized subset scoring at ~3% of compute for hundreds of tasks (Grad-TAG, KDD 2024), and online proximal affinity during training (ICLR 2025). Head selection should be an experiment, not a whiteboard argument.
  • Loss balancing first, gradient surgery second: uncertainty weighting (Kendall 2018) and GradNorm (2018) fix scale imbalance cheaply; PCGrad/CAGrad fix directional conflict; but a well-regularized plain sum often matches all of them (Kurin et al. 2022). Measure gradient conflict at the shared trunk before adopting machinery, and prefer the simplest optimizer that passes evaluation — it also aids reproducibility of versioned embeddings.
  • Many cheap, automatically generated heads scale surprisingly well: banks of random GVF/successor-measure prediction tasks yield frozen embeddings decodable by linear heads at near end-to-end performance (Proto-Value Networks 2023), and theory links such heads to the transition operator's dominant eigenspace (Lyle et al. 2021). A dense bank of cheap structural heads complements, rather than competes with, a few hand-picked decision heads.
  • Probing is both the training constraint and the evaluation contract: linear probes (Alain & Bengio 2016) operationalize 'linearly accessible', but accuracy-only probing is unstable — MDL/codelength probing (Voita & Titov 2020) measures how EASILY information is extractable and gives stable version-over-version comparisons, which is what a company-wide embedding SLA needs.
  • The information-bottleneck view supplies the compression side: the embedding should approach a minimal sufficient statistic for the UNION of its heads' targets (Alemi et al. 2017), with the bottleneck strength as an explicit dial between compactness/robustness and downstream coverage.
  • Head selection itself is learnable when hand-design stalls: bilevel/implicit-differentiation weighting of heads against a validation objective (AuxiLearn 2021, Auto-Lambda 2022), meta-gradient discovery of prediction questions (Veeriah et al. 2019), and evolutionary auxiliary-loss search with cross-environment transfer (A2LS 2022) form a maturity ladder from cheap (learned weights over fixed heads) to expensive (searching loss space).

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • Treat auxiliary-head selection as a measured task-affinity problem, not a design debate: enumerate ~20-30 candidate heads, run ONE instrumented all-heads training with TAG-style inter-task affinity logging, then use Grad-TAG-style linearized scoring to rank head SUBSETS against a held-out basket of downstream probe tasks (pricing, dispatch, ETA, repositioning, forecasting). Select the smallest subset that covers the basket, with a Tripuraneni-style diversity constraint rejecting near-duplicate heads.
  • Structure the head set in four families: (a) exogenous forecasting heads (next-bucket demand, supply arrivals, weather-conditioned volumes); (b) ACTION-CONDITIONED transition heads predicting next market state given the applied surge price/radius and incentive levels (BYOL-AC-style) — this is the non-negotiable answer to policy endogeneity, since policy-averaged forecasting provably collapses per-action structure; (c) decision-value heads (completion rate, pickup ETA, driver idle minutes, conversion at offered price) constrained to linear probes; (d) a cheap dense bank of random GVF/occupancy heads over the zone-time process (Proto-Value-Network-style) to densify latent geometry beyond what few hand-picked heads anchor.
  • Weighting and optimization: start with unitary scalarization plus Kendall-style uncertainty weighting to handle scale mismatch across heads. Log pairwise gradient cosines at the shared trunk throughout training; adopt PCGrad or CAGrad only if persistent conflict coincides with probe-scorecard degradation that a tuned equal-weight baseline cannot fix (per Kurin et al.'s counterpoint). If conflict is transient/regime-dependent, consider ICLR-2025-style selective task-group updates instead of permanent gradient surgery.
  • Make the evaluation contract MDL-based, not accuracy-based: version every embedding with a scorecard of online (prequential) codelengths for each downstream label family, computed with frozen encoder and standardized probe budgets. Gate releases on 'no codelength regression on any consumer's task family' — this is a stable, effort-aware backward-compatibility test that plain probe accuracy is not.
  • Add explicit sufficiency audits from the Rakelly framework: verify that decision-critical latent variables (price-elasticity proxies, supply-repositioning potential, cancellation risk drivers) are recoverable from the embedding, and never accept reconstruction loss as evidence of representation quality — reconstruction can be perfect while decision-relevant structure is linearly inaccessible.
  • Use a VIB-style stochastic bottleneck on the embedding with a modest β, swept against the MDL scorecard, to control dimensionality and noise robustness; this also gives a principled knob when downstream teams request smaller embeddings for latency-critical services.
  • Plan a maturity ladder for head selection automation: phase 1, fixed hand-designed heads with measured affinity-based pruning; phase 2, AuxiLearn/Auto-Lambda bilevel meta-learning of head weights against a multi-downstream validation objective (adapting their single-main-task formulation to a weighted basket); phase 3 (only with large spare compute), A2LS-style evolutionary search over the auxiliary-loss space for the marketplace domain.
  • Re-measure affinities on regime changes: Standley et al. showed task groupings shift with data volume and network capacity, so re-run the affinity analysis when onboarding new cities, changing zone granularity, or scaling the encoder — do not assume the head set chosen at launch remains optimal, and fold this re-measurement into the embedding versioning cadence.

8  Representations for Control & Causal / Counterfactual Inference

State of the art

Representation learning for control has evolved through three phases. First came reconstruction-based encoders (autoencoders on pixels), which were displaced around 2019-2021 by "self-predictive" objectives — DeepMDP, DBC-style bisimulation metrics, and SPR — that train an encoder so the latent state, conditioned on the ACTION, predicts the next latent state and the reward, discarding decision-irrelevant detail. In parallel, value-equivalent world models (MuZero, Dreamer, TD-MPC2) showed that an encoder need only preserve what is required to predict reward, value and policy under action sequences, and that planning in such latents is state-of-the-art for control; successor features separately showed how to factor dynamics from reward so one representation serves many reward functions. A consolidation phase (Ni et al. ICLR 2024; large-scale 2025 studies; the 2025 Echchahed & Castro survey, arXiv 2506.17518) established that the action-conditioned latent self-prediction ("ZP") loss plus reward prediction, with stop-gradient/EMA targets, is the common core that actually works, while pure metric losses are brittle. The causal thread runs alongside: because logged actions depend on the state (policy endogeneity), TARNet/CFR introduced balanced representations for counterfactual outcome prediction, DragonNet replaced balance with propensity-sufficiency, VCNet extended this to continuous treatments (directly analogous to a continuous surge multiplier), Delphic offline RL handles non-identifiable hidden confounding, and the OPE literature (Uehara-Shi-Kallus) specifies the logging (propensities, overlap/randomization) that any downstream evaluation needs. The 2025-2026 frontier is exactly this project's tension: learned world models degrade under policy-induced distribution shift (a formalized Lucas critique — performative prediction; DSGE-Gym 2026 shows off-path tail error ~40x on-path for Dreamer/transformer world models, largely repaired by training on structurally simulated counterfactual data). Open problems: objective mismatch between model loss and decision quality, representation collapse in self-prediction, the balance-vs-information tradeoff in shared embeddings, and OPE for continuous action spaces.

Key principles

  • Reconstruction is neither necessary nor sufficient for control: the field converged on action-conditioned latent self-prediction (predict z_{t+1} from z_t AND a_t) plus reward prediction as the core encoder objective (DeepMDP, SPR, Ni et al. 2024). A pure autoencoder organizes the latent by pixel/feature variance, not decision value — exactly the failure this project already anticipates.
  • Value equivalence is the strongest form of decision-awareness: MuZero/TD-MPC2 encoders keep ONLY what predicts reward, value and action outcomes, and this outperforms observation-predictive models for control; Dreamer-style observation prediction is a useful regularizer when rewards are sparse or many downstream tasks exist. For a shared company-wide embedding, a middle point (self-prediction + multiple KPI heads) beats either extreme.
  • Actions must be inputs to the representation learner, never treated as observed features: every control-grade objective conditions the transition on the action. If surge enters the encoder as just another input feature, the embedding confounds "market was hot" with "we priced high" and cannot answer counterfactuals.
  • Decouple dynamics from reward for cross-team transfer: successor features ψ(s,a) with task-specific linear weights w give provable zero-shot transfer across reward functions and yield exactly linear value readouts — the mathematically grounded version of the project's linear-probe constraint.
  • How the objective is optimized matters as much as the objective: stop-gradient/EMA target encoders prevent latent collapse (SPR, Ni et al. 2024); multi-step (K-step) latent rollouts beat one-step prediction; bisimulation-metric losses are brittle as the sole training signal and should at most be auxiliary (Luo et al. 2025 large-scale study).
  • Under policy-endogenous logging, the representation must support counterfactual queries, and there is a tradeoff: CFR-style balancing reduces policy-selection bias but destroys information; DragonNet's propensity-sufficiency view says instead to preserve exactly the confounding information and adjust with it. For a multi-consumer embedding, keep the shared representation information-rich (including a propensity head) and do balancing inside downstream causal heads.
  • Continuous control levers need continuous-treatment machinery: discretizing surge into treatment buckets produces discontinuous dose-response estimates; varying-coefficient heads (VCNet) keep the price-response curve smooth and identifiable.
  • Downstream benefit is certified by off-policy evaluation, not by reconstruction or forecasting error: IPS/DR estimators require logged propensities and overlap (some randomization of the levers). An embedding version should ship with an OPE-based scorecard of fixed reference policies plus a linear-probe suite on decision-relevant targets.
  • Lucas critique / performativity is a first-class design constraint: models fit on on-policy data are only disciplined where data were seen and collapse under policy regime shift (Perdomo et al. 2020; DSGE-Gym 2026). Mitigations that work: structural/simulator-generated counterfactual training data, explicit uncertainty over world models compatible with the data (delphic uncertainty), bounded exploration in the levers, and monitoring for performative drift after each policy change.
  • Pretrain-then-finetune with a frozen encoder is viable and industrially proven (SGI; DiDi's deployed dispatching value functions), but the probe/finetune protocol and the evaluation suite must be versioned together with the encoder weights.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • Train the encoder with a TD-MPC2/SPR-style composite objective, not an autoencoder: (1) K-step action-conditioned latent self-prediction with an EMA target encoder and stop-gradient (K≈4-8 time buckets), (2) reward/KPI prediction heads (completed trips, GMV, supply hours, cancellations), (3) optional TD value heads. Keep any observation decoder only as an offline diagnostic, never as the organizing loss.
  • Treat surge price, surge radius, incentives, and repositioning nudges as ACTIONS entering the latent transition model — z_{t+1} = f(z_t, a_t) — and never as covariates inside the state encoder. This is what makes the embedding answer counterfactuals ('demand at 1.4x surge') instead of memorizing the historical policy's correlations.
  • Add a DragonNet-style propensity head to the shared encoder: predict the logged surge/incentive action from z_t. It (a) certifies that confounder information survived compression, (b) supplies propensities for doubly-robust OPE, and (c) is itself a useful linear probe. For the continuous surge multiplier use a VCNet varying-coefficient outcome head to get smooth dose-response curves; do NOT discretize surge into buckets.
  • Do not put CFR-style balancing penalties on the shared embedding — balancing provably discards information (Shalit et al.'s bound) that other consumers (ETA, forecasting) need. Keep the shared z information-rich; apply balancing/weighting inside each downstream causal head that estimates effects.
  • Add a successor-feature head ψ(z, a) over a fixed basis of market features so any team's value function is a linear readout ψ·w. This operationalizes the linear-probe constraint, gives provable transfer when reward priorities change (e.g., growth vs profitability quarters), and lets you compare embedding versions by SF-probe quality.
  • Fix the logging contract now: log the propensity/parameters of every surge and incentive decision, and run bounded randomization (small jitter on surge in a rotating subset of zone-hours) to guarantee overlap. Per Uehara-Shi-Kallus, without logged propensities and overlap, no future OPE of embedding versions or policies is statistically valid.
  • Evaluate embeddings by decisions, not reconstructions: each versioned embedding ships with (a) a linear-probe suite on decision-relevant targets (short-horizon demand/supply, price elasticity, matching rate), (b) OPE scores (doubly-robust) of a fixed set of reference policies computed through the embedding, and (c) a denoising-factor-style diagnostic (Luo et al. 2025) checking that known-irrelevant telemetry is filtered out.
  • Defend against the Lucas critique explicitly (DSGE-Gym 2026 result): maintain a structural marketplace simulator (queueing/matching + simple rider/driver choice models) and mix its counterfactual regimes (extreme surge, supply shocks, new-policy rollouts) into encoder training data; report off-path prediction error separately from on-path error in every embedding release; monitor performative drift after each downstream policy launch and schedule retraining accordingly (Perdomo et al.).
  • Quantify confounding risk with delphic-uncertainty-style ensembles: train several world models consistent with the logged data and flag zone-hours where their counterfactual predictions diverge; downstream pricing should fall back to conservative policies there, and those zone-hours are the highest-value targets for randomized exploration.
  • Sequence the rollout like SGI/DiDi: pretrain the encoder offline on historical zone-time trajectories, validate with the probe+OPE suite, freeze and version it, let downstream teams consume via linear heads first (cheap, comparable, auditable), and only later allow selective fine-tuning through decision-focused (regret) losses for the highest-value consumers such as surge optimization.

9  Industrial Embedding Systems at Marketplaces & Two-Sided Platforms

State of the art

Industrial embedding systems at marketplaces evolved in three waves. Wave 1 (2016-2019) established that entity embeddings trained on interaction logs, served via ANN retrieval, ship real revenue: Airbnb's listing embeddings (skip-gram over click sessions with booking-aware objectives), YouTube's two-tower retrieval, and Pinterest's PinSage (graph convolutions over billions of nodes) — all consumed as precomputed vectors from a feature store. In parallel, ride-hailing platforms (Uber, DiDi) shipped spatiotemporal representations: bucketize-and-embed geo/time features (DeepETA), coarse-coded hierarchical spatial encodings in dispatch value networks, and LSTM-autoencoder "city state" features feeding demand forecasters — the direct ancestors of a market-state embedding. Wave 2 (2020-2023) industrialized the operating model: feature stores (Uber Michelangelo Palette, Lyft Feature Service, DoorDash's Redis-based store) became the distribution fabric for embeddings with offline/online parity, defined refresh cadences, and entity-keyed serving; Pinterest's ItemSage proved that one multi-task embedding serving many surfaces beats per-team embeddings on both metrics and maintenance cost; and Stanford/Pinterest's Learning Backward Compatible Embeddings formalized version governance so consumer teams need not retrain when the producer retrains. Wave 3 (2024-2026) is consolidation into foundation-model-style central representations: OmniSearchSage (multi-entity, multi-task, co-trained for compatibility with existing embedding spaces, 300k QPS), DoorDash's DashCLIP (LLM-curated labels replacing policy-endogenous engagement supervision), Netflix's personalization foundation model (one autoregressive model whose embeddings, fine-tunes, and direct predictions are consumed company-wide with explicit stability handling across retrains), and Airbnb's 2026 production embedding-based retrieval handling dynamic two-sided inventory. Open problems that recur across these systems and map directly onto this project: keeping the latent space organized by decision value rather than reconstruction fidelity, de-biasing training signal that was generated under the platform's own control policies, and managing representation churn/version compatibility for dozens of downstream consumers.

Key principles

  • Objective beats architecture: every successful industrial embedding modified its training objective to encode the business event that downstream decisions hinge on (Airbnb's booked-listing global context and in-market negatives; ItemSage's purchase heads; Yi et al.'s bias-corrected softmax). Pure reconstruction or generic co-occurrence consistently underperforms decision-aligned objectives downstream.
  • Multi-task, multi-surface training is the shipped standard for embeddings with many consumers: a single embedding trained against several downstream objectives simultaneously (ItemSage, OmniSearchSage, Netflix FM) outperforms per-team embeddings, helps sparse-label tasks most, and slashes maintenance cost — validating auxiliary prediction heads as the central design element, not an add-on.
  • Linear accessibility is engineered on purpose: two-tower dot-product compatibility, linear probes, and ANN-consumable geometries are chosen so that downstream consumption is cheap and simple; if a consumer needs a deep model to extract signal from the embedding, industry treats that as a defect of the embedding.
  • Bias in the training signal must be corrected explicitly or it is inherited by every consumer: sampling-bias correction (YouTube), position/selection bias handling, causal separation of forecasting from lever-effect estimation (DoorDash), and LLM-curated labels replacing engagement logs (DashCLIP) are all responses to the same problem this project faces with surge-endogenous data.
  • Spatiotemporal inputs are quantized then embedded: production systems (DeepETA, DiDi value networks) bucketize continuous features and embed the buckets, and encode location at multiple spatial resolutions simultaneously (multi-precision geohash/H3, CMAC coarse coding) — this gives robustness, smoothness, and cross-city transfer that raw continuous features and single-resolution zone IDs do not.
  • Embeddings are feature-store artifacts, not model internals: they are keyed by entity, stored with offline/online parity and point-in-time-correct history for training joins, refreshed on a declared cadence (daily batch for slow entities, streaming for fast market state), and accessed config-driven by consumer teams (Palette, Lyft Feature Service, Uber TTE).
  • Version governance is a first-class engineering problem: retraining rotates the latent space and silently breaks consumer models, so shipped systems use learned backward-alignment transforms (BC-Aligner), co-training new versions for compatibility with existing spaces (OmniSearchSage), incremental training with warm starts to limit churn (Netflix), and parallel serving of adjacent versions during migration.
  • Evaluation is two-layer and consumer-centric: intrinsic metrics (retrieval hit-rate, probe accuracy on downstream labels) gate iteration speed, but promotion requires wins in consumer-team A/B tests; embeddings survive organizationally only when multiple downstream teams can demonstrate lift from them.
  • Cold start and regime shift are handled by content/attribute-based encoding rather than pure ID memorization: Airbnb's attribute averaging, PinSage/ItemSage content features, Netflix metadata tokens — for market state this means encoding zones by observable features (demographics, POI, weather, historical dynamics) so new zones, cities, and regimes generalize.
  • Serving constraints shape the encoder: millisecond latency budgets drove linear attention (DeepETA), precomputation plus ANN lookup (all retrieval systems), and offline batch inference at billions-of-entities scale (PinSage MapReduce); the embedding's refresh latency must match the fastest consumer's decision cadence.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • Make multi-task heads the primary training signal, not auxiliary: train the zone-time encoder jointly on demand forecast, supply/availability forecast, conversion rate, ETA residual, and incentive/surge response heads (ItemSage/OmniSearchSage pattern), keeping heads linear to enforce linear accessibility. Weight tasks so sparse-but-valuable labels (incentive response) borrow strength from dense ones (demand). Retain a small reconstruction term only as regularization.
  • Encode inputs the way deployed ride-hailing models do: bucketize all continuous features and embed the buckets; represent location with multi-resolution hierarchical embeddings (e.g., H3 at 2-3 resolutions, summed or concatenated) rather than a flat zone-ID table; add calendar/event/weather embeddings; add a functional-similarity graph over zones (DMVST-Net semantic view) so behaviorally similar zones share statistics. This buys robustness, smoothness, and cross-city transfer (DiDi's CMAC evidence).
  • Treat action-conditioning as a schema requirement from day one: log surge price, surge radius, and incentive levers alongside every state snapshot; train action-conditioned heads (predict outcome given state AND action) rather than marginal heads, so the embedding separates market state from policy artifacts. Follow DoorDash's split: embedding feeds forecasts, causal lever effects are estimated separately (with experiments or de-confounding), and decisions come from an optimization layer consuming both. Consider LLM/synthetic labeling (DashCLIP pattern) for supervision channels not contaminated by the control policy.

10  Spatiotemporal, Urban & Graph Embeddings

State of the art

Spatiotemporal and urban/graph embedding research has evolved along three converging tracks that map directly onto a per-hex-per-time market-state object. (1) Spatiotemporal GNN forecasting began with DCRNN, STGCN, and Graph WaveNet (2018-19), which established that a city is a graph and that spatial dependency should be baked in via graph convolution/diffusion over an adjacency structure — with Graph WaveNet's self-adaptive adjacency showing the decisive lesson that the *learned* dependency graph beats the hand-specified one. DiDi's ST-MGCN carried this into deployed ride-hailing demand forecasting with multiple graphs (spatial neighborhood, functional similarity, transport connectivity). A counter-current then emerged: STID (CIKM 2022) and STAEformer (CIKM 2023) showed that most of the value of these architectures is captured by learned per-node and per-time-slot *identity embeddings* attached to simple MLPs/vanilla Transformers, while BigST (VLDB 2024) made STGNNs linear-complexity and scalable to 100k nodes — i.e., citywide hex grids. (2) Urban region embedding grew from random-walk/skip-gram methods on mobility flow graphs (HDGE 2017, ZE-Mob 2018 — the latter using PMI of origin-destination co-occurrence in taxi trips) through multi-graph attention fusion (MGFN 2022) to task-adaptation-aware methods: HREP (AAAI 2023) introduced per-task prompt embeddings on top of a shared heterogeneous-relation embedding, and the 2025-26 generation (GURPP, FlexiReg, UrbanVerse) does graph-based pre-training plus prompting for cross-task and cross-city transfer; hex2vec/srai provides the production-grade H3-hexagon static-feature embedding tooling. (3) Trajectory embeddings (t2vec 2018, TrajCL 2023) contributed the seq2seq-denoising and contrastive-augmentation recipes for encoding movement, and spatiotemporal self-supervision (ST-SSL, AAAI 2023) showed auxiliary contrastive tasks that explicitly model spatial and temporal heterogeneity improve robustness on skewed distributions — exactly the surge-tail regime. Since 2024 the frontier is urban foundation models: UniST (masked ST modeling + memory-based prompts), UrbanGPT (ST encoder + LLM instruction tuning), OpenCity (graph-Transformer pre-trained across cities, zero-shot with scaling laws), and 2026's UrbanFM (billion-scale corpus, minimal-inductive-bias attention, largest ST benchmark to date). Open problems: policy-endogeneity of logged urban data is barely addressed (models treat demand as exogenous, not action-conditioned), evaluation is still per-task rather than probe-based, and dynamic (time-varying) region embeddings remain much less mature than static ones.

Key principles

  • Spatial dependency should enter the encoder as an explicit graph operator (diffusion/message passing over the hex adjacency), but the adjacency should be partly LEARNED: Graph WaveNet, STAEformer, and OpenCity all show self-adaptive/attention-derived dependency matrices beat purely geographic ones because true influence (e.g., surge spillover, substitutable pickup zones) is not purely distance-based. Best practice is hybrid: fixed hex-neighbor graph + learned adaptive graph + optionally functional-similarity and connectivity graphs (ST-MGCN).
  • Learned identity embeddings are load-bearing: STID and STAEformer demonstrate that per-node (hex) and per-time-slot (time-of-day, day-of-week) embedding tables, jointly trained, capture most spatial-temporal distinguishability — any market-state encoder should include them, and they double as a cheap versioned 'static hex embedding' product.
  • Reconstruction alone under-organizes the latent space; auxiliary self-supervised tasks that model HETEROGENEITY (spatial: different hexes have skewed distributions; temporal: peak vs off-peak regimes) reshape representations toward decision-relevant structure (ST-SSL). Contrastive objectives with domain-appropriate augmentations (TrajCL) and masked spatiotemporal modeling (UniST) are the two dominant recipes.
  • Multi-view / multi-graph fusion beats any single data source: region embeddings built from mobility flows + POI/land-use + neighbor structure (HDGE, MGFN, HREP, hex2vec) transfer to more downstream tasks than single-view embeddings; attention-based fusion with per-view embeddings preserved until late fusion is the standard architecture.
  • Task adaptation should be cheap and explicit: the field converged on 'frozen shared embedding + small per-task prompt/adapter' (HREP, GURPP, FlexiReg, UniST) rather than per-task fine-tuning — this is precisely the mechanism that keeps a company-wide embedding backward compatible while letting pricing vs dispatch vs ETA each get a tailored view.
  • Downstream evaluation of region embeddings is standardized as LINEAR probes / shallow regressors on held-out urban indicators (crime, check-ins, land use, flow prediction) — validating the project's plan to constrain auxiliary heads to linear probes so decision-relevant information is linearly accessible.
  • Long-history context can be precomputed: BigST separates an offline long-sequence temporal encoder (thousands of past steps compressed into low-dim representations) from a cheap online spatial propagation step — a serving pattern that matches an embedding service with strict latency budgets.
  • Cross-city / zero-shot generalization comes from scale plus weak inductive bias: OpenCity and UrbanFM show pre-training across many cities with normalized inputs yields transferable spatiotemporal representations with observable scaling laws, while UrbanVerse shows region-conditioned + task-conditioned generation transfers across both cities and tasks.
  • Trajectory-level encoders (t2vec, TrajCL) show robustness must be trained in, not hoped for: denoising/augmentation-invariance objectives (downsampling, noise, distortion) produce embeddings stable under data-quality variation — the analogue for market state is augmentation over missing zones, sensor dropouts, and event shocks.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • Architect the encoder as: (a) learned hex-ID + time-of-day + day-of-week embedding tables (STID/STAEformer lesson), (b) a temporal module over each hex's recent history, and (c) a spatial mixing layer over a HYBRID graph — fixed H3 neighbor adjacency plus a Graph WaveNet-style learned adaptive adjacency plus optional functional-similarity and road-connectivity graphs (ST-MGCN). The learned adjacency is where non-geographic surge spillovers (driver reachability, substitutable zones) get captured; publish it as an interpretable artifact for the pricing team.
  • Ablate ruthlessly against the STID baseline (hex-ID + time-ID embeddings + MLP): if the GNN does not beat it on downstream probes, the spatial machinery is not earning its complexity. This is a one-week experiment that de-risks the whole architecture choice.
  • Adopt ST-SSL-style heterogeneity-aware auxiliary tasks alongside the planned linear-probe prediction heads: a spatial contrastive task (cluster hexes into soft regimes) and a temporal regime-discrimination task (peak/off-peak/surge). These specifically prevent the failure mode you identified — reconstruction averaging away tail regimes that matter most for surge decisions.
  • Use masked spatiotemporal modeling (UniST's masking menu: random, block/'tube', temporal, and spatial-region masking) as the core pre-training objective rather than plain autoencoding; it doubles as robustness training for missing zones and telemetry gaps, and pairs naturally with your linear-probe auxiliary heads.
  • Handle policy-endogeneity explicitly, because this literature does not: log surge price and surge radius as INPUTS to the encoder (action-conditioned representation) or train with them masked and probed, so the embedding does not silently entangle 'high demand' with 'we surged here'. None of the surveyed ST models condition on control levers — this is your novelty and your biggest risk; borrow action-conditioning from decision-focused RL representation work rather than expecting it from ST forecasting papers.
  • Serve with the BigST split: precompute each hex's long-horizon temporal summary offline (daily/hourly batch), run only linear-complexity spatial propagation plus latest-bucket fusion online. This matches an embedding-service latency budget at 10k-100k hex scale.
  • Distribute via the freeze-and-prompt pattern (HREP/GURPP/FlexiReg/UniST): version-freeze the backbone embedding, and give each consumer team a small learned prompt/adapter head trained against the frozen embedding. Backward compatibility then reduces to keeping the frozen backbone stable, and per-task prompts absorb drift — re-train prompts, not the backbone, between minor versions.
  • Build the evaluation harness as a battery of frozen-embedding linear probes over ALL downstream tasks (demand/supply forecasting at multiple horizons, ETA residuals, completion rate, incentive response, plus static probes like POI/land-use recovery), in the spirit of the region-embedding literature's standard protocol and UrbanFM's EvalST; gate embedding version releases on probe-regression tests rather than a single reconstruction metric.
  • Concatenate a static channel from hex2vec/srai (OSM tags on your exact H3 grid) with the learned dynamic state; it is nearly free, adds urban-function priors the trip data cannot see for new/sparse zones, and srai accelerates prototyping.
  • Pre-train across all your cities jointly with per-city input normalization (OpenCity/UrbanFM recipe) and validate zero-shot transfer to a held-out city — this both improves data efficiency in small markets and gives you a launch-day embedding for new cities; consider warm-starting from OpenCity's open weights for the traffic-like channels.
  • If driver/rider trajectories are available, add a pooled trajectory-embedding channel per hex-time bucket using the TrajCL contrastive recipe with augmentations chosen to encode invariances downstream models should have (GPS noise, sampling-rate variation) — supply behavior (cruising vs repositioning) is market state that OD counts miss.

11  Evaluating Embedding Quality & Operating Embeddings Over Time

State of the art

This area answers two operational questions: (1) how do you know an embedding is good before and after downstream teams consume it, and (2) how do you keep shipping new encoder versions without breaking every consumer. Evaluation evolved from single-task linear probes (the de facto SSL standard) to critiques showing probe accuracy is protocol-sensitive and conflates "information exists" with "information is accessible" — driving information-theoretic reframings (MDL probes, readout model switching) that measure description length and data-efficiency rather than raw accuracy. In parallel, a family of label-free metrics emerged that predict downstream performance from embedding geometry alone: alignment/uniformity (2020), effective rank / RankMe (2023), eigenspectrum decay alpha-ReQ (2022), LiDAR (2024), and intrinsic dimension (2019); 2025 work (including a Dec-2025 persistent-homology metric) shows each fails in some regimes — especially class imbalance and domain shift — so the current SOTA position is a dashboard of complementary metrics validated against a fixed multi-task benchmark suite, the pattern institutionalized by VTAB and MTEB and scaled by MMTEB (ICLR 2025) with correlation-based task downsampling. The operations track began with Backward-Compatible Training (Shen et al., CVPR 2020), which made backfill-free encoder upgrades possible, and progressed through forward-compatible training with side-information (Apple, CVPR 2022), regression/negative-flip minimization (Amazon, CVPR 2021), theoretically grounded stationary representations via fixed simplex classifiers (CVPR 2024), and 2025 work from Snap/Yale using hyperbolic entailment cones and from the continual-learning community using query-drift compensation to keep old indexes usable without re-embedding. Drift monitoring rests on Failing Loudly (NeurIPS 2019) — two-sample tests on dimensionality-reduced representations plus domain classifiers — now productized in Evidently/Arize/WhyLabs. Matryoshka Representation Learning (NeurIPS 2022), adopted by OpenAI and Google embedding APIs, resolved the dimensionality trade-off by nesting granularities in one vector. Open problems: no label-free metric is universally reliable; the compatibility-plasticity trade-off (alignment to old embeddings caps improvement); and principled evaluation under policy-endogenous distribution shift remains essentially unstudied — directly relevant when surge control both consumes and perturbs the embedding.

Key principles

  • Probe accuracy conflates existence with accessibility of information: MDL-based measures (description length, prequential coding, readout model switching) quantify how cheaply information can be extracted — which is what downstream consumers actually pay for — and are far better at separating real signal from probe memorization than raw linear-probe accuracy.
  • Label-free geometric/spectral metrics (alignment-uniformity, effective rank/RankMe, eigenspectrum decay alpha, LiDAR, intrinsic dimension, topological persistence) are cheap continuous training-time monitors that catch representation collapse before any downstream model exists — but every one of them fails in some regime (imbalance, domain shift, non-vision data), so they are guardrails and selection heuristics, never training objectives or sole release gates.
  • Representation quality is only defined relative to a fixed, versioned, diverse multi-task benchmark (VTAB/MTEB pattern): frozen encoder, constrained adaptation budget, limited-label splits, and correlation-based task downsampling to keep evaluation cheap enough to run on every candidate — the benchmark suite is the embedding's contract with the organization.
  • Report data-efficiency curves, not point accuracies: how well a probe does with 1% and 10% of labels (equivalently, area under the online-coding learning curve) is the property that transfers to new downstream use-cases and small-sample geo-zones.
  • Compatibility is a trainable property, achieved three ways with different costs: train it in (BCT influence loss, fixed stationary/simplex target geometry), transform it in (old-to-new mappings with side-information, FCT), or compensate at serving time (query-drift projection). The success metric is cross-version performance (old heads on new embeddings) and per-consumer negative-flip rate — not new-model average accuracy.
  • Regression is a first-class release metric: a version that improves on average can still silently break individual consumers; negative flips must be measured per downstream task and gated per consumer.
  • Capacity should be nested, not fixed: Matryoshka-style training makes one embedding serve consumers with wildly different latency/storage budgets via prefix truncation, and makes the dimension count part of a graceful versioned contract rather than a breaking change.
  • Drift monitoring belongs on the embedding itself: two-sample tests on (reduced) embedding dimensions, domain classifiers, and centroid distances detect distribution shift without labels — but statistical drift is not harm; alerts must be paired with downstream proxy metrics, and in systems where actions feed back into data (surge), policy-induced drift must be distinguished from environment-induced drift.
  • No single metric is universal (metric correlations reverse across datasets): operate a validated panel — spectral + probe-based + drift — and re-validate the panel against your own downstream tasks whenever data distribution or policy changes.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • Build a frozen 'Market-State Eval Suite' (VTAB/MTEB pattern) before shipping v1: 8-12 fixed downstream tasks with locked splits (surge-outcome regression, demand and supply forecasting at multiple horizons, ETA error, dispatch match quality, incentive uplift, anomaly/event detection), evaluated with frozen-encoder linear probes; score every encoder candidate on the full suite and prune redundant tasks via inter-task correlation (MMTEB's downsampling) to keep it cheap enough for nightly runs.
  • Evaluate with data-efficiency, not just accuracy: for each task report probe performance at 1%/10%/100% labels and an MDL/online-coding score; this directly certifies the project's design goal that decision-relevant information be linearly and cheaply accessible, and it predicts how the embedding will serve new consumers and sparse geo-zones.
  • Periodically run a capacity-varying readout (readout model switching): the gap between linear-probe and small-MLP performance per task is the diagnostic for whether to add another auxiliary linear head to the encoder (information exists but is not linearly organized) versus accept a nonlinear consumer.
  • Instrument encoder training with a label-free metric panel — RankMe effective rank, alpha eigenspectrum decay, alignment/uniformity on positive pairs (same zone adjacent buckets / perturbed views), and optionally LiDAR using those positive groups — as collapse alarms and for hyperparameter sweeps; but first validate each metric's correlation against the Eval Suite on marketplace data (Tsitsulin's reversal warning applies doubly to non-vision tabular/spatio-temporal data), and never optimize them directly.
  • Train with Matryoshka nesting (e.g., 32/64/128/256 dims): low-latency pricing and dispatch consume a short prefix, forecasting consumes the full vector, and the prefix hierarchy is published as part of the embedding contract; use intrinsic-dimension estimates to pick the smallest prefix.
  • Treat every encoder retrain as a compatibility event with explicit gates: (a) add a BCT-style influence loss against v(n) probe heads — or better, adopt a fixed stationary target geometry (d-Simplex-style) so all future versions aim at the same coordinates; (b) gate releases on cross-version probe performance (v(n) heads reading v(n+1) embeddings) and per-consumer negative-flip rate (PC-training's metric), e.g., 'no zone-hour where surge decisions regress'; (c) for noisy/sparse zones prefer uncertainty-weighted alignment (2025 hyperbolic BCT) to avoid inheriting old-version errors.
  • Preserve historical embeddings across versions cheaply: store FCT-style side-information alongside each logged zone-time embedding so backtesting and forecasting archives can be mapped forward with a learned transformation instead of re-encoding raw history; for frequent minor fine-tunes between major versions, use query-drift compensation so consumers stay on one interface.
  • Deploy embedding drift monitoring from day one (Failing Loudly recipe, as productized by Evidently/Arize): per-dimension two-sample tests with multiple-testing correction on PCA-reduced embeddings, a domain classifier per region, centroid drift per geo-zone, plus monitoring of probe-head output distributions; log the embedding version with every downstream prediction for regression attribution.
  • Because surge price/radius are control levers, add a causal triage layer to monitoring and evaluation: condition drift analysis on the logged action (so a surge-policy change is not misread as market drift), and include action-conditioned probes in the Eval Suite (predict outcome given state embedding AND logged action) so the benchmark rewards causally useful state information rather than policy-artifact shortcuts.
  • Operationalize versioning like an API: semantic-versioned embeddings in the feature store, dual-index/shadow rollout of each new version with consumer-specific regression dashboards before cutover, and a deprecation window during which both versions are served — compatibility training makes this window cheap, and negative-flip gates make it safe.

12  Causal Inference & Experiment Design Under Marketplace Interference

This chapter closes a gap identified by the completeness critic; it was researched by a dedicated gap-fill agent.

State of the art

Causal inference under marketplace interference has evolved from documenting the problem to prescribing designs and estimators that are now standard at ride-hailing and delivery platforms. The field's starting point is that surge-style levers violate SUTVA twice over: spatially (a price/radius change in one zone moves drivers and riders across zone boundaries — an equilibrium effect formalized by the wild-goose-chase and spatio-temporal-pricing literatures) and temporally (the platform state is a Markov chain, so treatments carry over). First-generation responses were design-side: switchback experiments that randomize the whole market over time blocks (Bojinov-Simchi-Levi-Zhao; refined by Hu-Wager's burn-in analysis showing naive switchbacks lose a T^{1/6} rate factor to carryover bias) and two-sided randomization for cross-side interference (Johari et al., who prove the sign and regime-dependence of naive A/B bias). Second-generation work is estimator-side and model-based: Farias et al.'s Differences-in-Q's estimator uses a learned Q-function on the platform's Markov state to cut interference bias to second order with exponentially less variance than unbiased OPE; Luo et al.'s varying-coefficient decision process decomposes ATE into direct and indirect (spillover) effects on real ride-hailing experiments. Current SOTA (2024-2026) unifies space and time: clustered switchback designs with near-minimax Horvitz-Thompson guarantees (Jia-Kallus-Yu, extended in 2025 to non-stationary, treatment-independent random interference graphs motivated by driver movement), MSE-optimal spatial cluster construction via causal graph cuts validated on city-scale ridesharing simulators (Zhu et al., ICML 2025; related work shows spatially randomized designs can beat global rollouts for policy evaluation, Yang et al., AOAS 2026), permutation-invariant neural OPE that learns the mean-field interference function from data (Causal Deepsets), and design-based frameworks for arbitrary spillover/carryover with software support (Mukaigawara-Imai et al.'s geocausal). Open problems: jointly optimal spatio-temporal cluster/block sizing under unknown mixing times, interference-aware inference when the lever is continuous (dose-response rather than binary), endogenous interference graphs that respond to treatment, and integrating these estimators into always-on release gates rather than one-off studies. For this project the literature carries a blunt upstream message: the causal content of a market-state embedding is bounded by the randomization actually present in the logs, so the experimentation substrate is part of the representation-learning system, not an afterthought.

Key principles

  • Interference bias is first-order and sign-varying, not noise: naive unit-level A/B in a two-sided market can overstate or understate effects depending on whether the market is demand- or supply-constrained (Johari et al.), so any embedding head trained or validated on naively analyzed logs encodes a biased dose-response. Causal usefulness downstream requires interference-aware estimands baked in from the start.
  • There is a menu of estimands — direct (own-zone), indirect/spillover (neighbor-zone), total/global (market-wide policy contrast), and continuous dose-response — and different downstream consumers need different ones: pricing needs own-zone dose-response, repositioning and dispatch need spillover terms, and executive go/no-go decisions need the total effect. An embedding that only supports one estimand under-serves the platform.
  • Identifiability is purchased by design, not by modeling: the randomization unit must match the interference structure — time blocks longer than the system's mixing time (with burn-in discarding) for temporal carryover, graph-cut spatial clusters that sever driver-flow edges for spatial spillover, and clustered switchbacks when both operate. Without such variation in the logs, action-conditioned representations are unidentifiable no matter the architecture.
  • Learned value/Q-functions are the bridge between representation learning and interference-robust evaluation: the Differences-in-Q's insight is that a Q-head on the (embedded) market state converts interference bias into a second-order term while slashing variance relative to unbiased OPE — so a Q-head is simultaneously an auxiliary training signal and the platform's best experiment-analysis tool.
  • Small, always-on randomized perturbations of continuous levers ('experimenting in equilibrium') identify local dose-response gradients at vanishing business cost, and are the cheapest permanent data source for causal heads — dithering is a logging requirement, not an experiment to be scheduled.
  • Mean-field / permutation-invariant aggregation of neighbor states and actions is the right inductive bias for spatial interference: outcomes depend on one's own state plus an exchangeable summary of neighbors (Causal Deepsets), so the encoder should ingest neighborhood aggregates and spillover heads should pool neighbor embeddings permutation-invariantly rather than assuming a fixed adjacency ordering.
  • Control design and inference design are coupled: spatially/temporally smooth pricing (Ma-Fang-Parkes) both prevents the equilibrium unraveling (surge chasing, wild-goose-chase regimes) that generates the worst spillovers and keeps the system in a locally smooth response regime where dose-response estimation and mean-field approximations are valid. Constraining the lever makes its effects learnable.
  • Evaluation of the embedding itself is subject to the same interference as the policies it powers: an embedding-version rollout changes pricing/dispatch market-wide, so promotion gates must use switchback or cluster-randomized designs analyzed with design-based (truncated Horvitz-Thompson), Q-based (DQ), or decomposition-based (VCDP direct/indirect) estimators — never user-level difference-in-means.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • LOGGING SPEC — record identification-critical fields per zone x time-bucket: realized surge multiplier and radius, the policy's intended (pre-dithering) value, the dithering draw and its seed, the assignment mechanism and cluster-map version, cluster-level propensities, and a permutation-invariant snapshot of neighbor zones' states and levers. Without logged propensities and neighbor levers, none of the interference-aware estimators (HT, DQ, VCDP, Causal Deepsets) can be computed later.
  • LOGGING SPEC — run a permanent clustered-switchback substrate (Jia-Kallus-Yu design): spatial clusters derived by causal graph cuts (Zhu et al. 2025) on the empirically measured driver-flow / demand-correlation graph, re-derived quarterly as mobility patterns shift; time blocks sized above the estimated supply-mixing time (start at 60-120 min, tuned via Bojinov et al.'s data-driven carryover procedure) with Hu-Wager burn-in periods flagged in the logs so training and estimation can discard post-flip transients.
  • LOGGING SPEC — keep always-on small-amplitude randomized dithering of the continuous levers (Wager-Xu 'experimenting in equilibrium'): e.g., +/-2-5% multiplicative jitter on surge price and +/-1 step on surge radius, independently randomized per zone-bucket within policy-safe bounds. This is the primary identification source for the dose-response heads and costs vanishingly little revenue; treat its removal as a breaking change to the embedding pipeline.
  • HEAD DESIGN — four interference-aware auxiliary heads on the encoder, all trained only on randomized/dithered data windows: (1) an own-zone dose-response head predicting outcome deltas as a function of own lever (Munro-Wager-Xu direct effect); (2) a spillover head predicting neighbor-zone outcome response to own-zone lever using permutation-invariant pooling of neighbor embeddings (Causal Deepsets architecture; VCDP indirect effect); (3) a TD-trained zone-level Q/value head over the platform Markov state — this head doubles as the engine of the DQ estimator (Farias et al.) and should be a versioned, officially supported output of the embedding service; (4) a cluster-level propensity head reproducing the logging policy's lever distribution, for IPS/DR-style corrections at the correct (cluster, not unit) granularity.
  • HEAD DESIGN — encode regime and smoothness structure: include distance-to-wild-goose-chase-regime indicators (idle-driver density, pickup ETA percentiles per Castillo et al.) as encoder inputs and probe targets, and add a spatial/temporal smoothness regularizer on any surge recommendations derived from the embedding, consistent with Ma-Fang-Parkes — this both curbs the driver-flow spillovers that inflate interference and keeps the response surface in the locally smooth regime the causal heads assume.
  • EVALUATION PROTOCOL — embedding-version promotion gates must be interference-aware: online tests of a new embedding version (or any surge policy it powers) run as clustered switchbacks, never rider/driver-level splits (Johari et al. bias results); analyze every test with a three-estimator scorecard — truncated Horvitz-Thompson (design-based, Jia-Kallus-Yu), Differences-in-Q's via the Q-head (model-based, low variance), and VCDP direct/indirect decomposition — and gate on the total (global) effect while reporting DE and IE separately so consumers like repositioning see spillover impact explicitly. Divergence among the three estimators is itself a red flag to investigate before promotion.
  • EVALUATION PROTOCOL — offline pre-gates use interference-aware OPE: score candidate embedding versions and surge policies from logs with Causal Deepsets / VCDP-based OPE (and geocausal as an assumption-light cross-check), reporting direct, indirect, and total estimands plus sensitivity to the assumed interference neighborhood; only candidates passing the offline scorecard graduate to the (expensive, market-level) clustered-switchback slot, integrating with the release-gate ladder from the evaluation/versioning workstream.
  • EVALUATION PROTOCOL — for changes touching both sides of the market (rider-facing and driver-facing consumers of the embedding), use two-sided randomization (Johari et al.) as the fallback when a switchback slot is unavailable, and log market balance (demand- vs supply-constrained regime) at test time because the sign of residual bias depends on it.
  • COST AND GOVERNANCE — budget the substrate explicitly: reserve a small standing fraction of zone-bucket cells for the clustered-switchback rotation and accept the dithering variance as a permanent data-collection cost; version the cluster maps, block lengths, and dithering amplitudes alongside embedding versions so that every embedding release records exactly which randomization regime generated its training data — this is the backward-compatibility analogue for causal validity.

13  Temporal Distribution Shift: Generalization & Continual Adaptation of the Encoder

This chapter closes a gap identified by the completeness critic; it was researched by a dedicated gap-fill agent.

State of the art

Temporal distribution shift research has moved through three overlapping waves. First came benchmarking: Wild-Time (NeurIPS 2022) established that on real timestamped data, models lose roughly 20% performance going from in-distribution to future-time evaluation, and that 13 methods spanning domain generalization, continual learning, SSL, and ensembling barely beat plain ERM — establishing temporally forward splits (Eval-Fix, Eval-Stream) as the mandatory evaluation contract. The second wave is temporal domain generalization (TDG) proper: treat time as a domain index and extrapolate the model to the near future — DRAIN forecasts next-period network parameters with a Bayesian recurrent graph generator; prompting-based TDG and TEA (EMNLP 2025) make this scale to large backbones by extrapolating only prompts or weight-averaging coefficients; FreKoo (2025) extrapolates only the low-frequency spectral component of parameter trajectories via Koopman operators; CODA sidesteps the model entirely by simulating future training data; Drift-Resilient TabPFN (NeurIPS 2024) bakes gradually-shifting structural causal models into a prior-fitted transformer so drift adaptation happens in-context in one forward pass. The third wave is a sobriety check plus operational adaptation: 'Temporal Generalization: A Reality Check' (2025) finds that across large-scale monthly/yearly tasks no interpolation or extrapolation method consistently beats simply using the latest warm-started model, echoing the LLM continual pre-training result (Ibrahim et al. 2024) that LR re-warming + re-decay + replay matches full retraining at a fraction of the compute — with Ash & Adams' shrink-and-perturb as the known fix for the warm-start generalization pathology. On the adaptation side, Tent-style test-time entropy minimization is powerful but the TTAB benchmark (ICML 2023) documents its instability (online batch dependency, hyperparameter fragility), ARM offers a safer meta-learned adapt-from-unlabeled-context alternative, Proceed (KDD 2025) maps estimated drift to parameter offsets via a learned generator, and CaSSLe shows continual SSL needs cross-version representation distillation to avoid forgetting. Open problems: no TDG method reliably wins over frequent warm-started retraining at scale; disentangling environment drift from self-induced (performative) drift is unsolved; and the interaction between fast adaptation and cross-version embedding compatibility is essentially unstudied.

Key principles

  • The near future is the only test set that matters: model selection, hyperparameter tuning, and probe evaluation must all use temporally forward splits (Wild-Time Eval-Fix / Eval-Stream); random splits anywhere in the encoder pipeline silently overstate serving performance by ~20%.
  • Simple frequent warm-started retraining is the champion to beat: across large-scale evaluations (Reality Check 2025; Ibrahim et al. 2024) no parameter interpolation/extrapolation method consistently outperforms 'serve the latest warm-started model' — any TDG machinery must first beat that baseline on your own forward splits.
  • Warm-starting has a known generalization pathology and a cheap cure: apply shrink-and-perturb and/or LR re-warming + re-decay + replay at every scheduled retrain, or the encoder ossifies while training loss looks fine.
  • Separate slow and fast parameters: keep a frozen or slowly-updated trunk (stable latent geometry, backward compatibility) and confine temporal adaptation to small fast components — prompts, normalization/affine layers, per-zone identity embeddings, low-rank adaptation offsets (Prompting-TDG, Tent, Proceed).
  • Extrapolate structure, suppress noise: forecast only the low-frequency component of drift (seasonality, city growth) and regularize against chasing high-frequency transients (FreKoo, TEA, DRAIN) — trajectory-aware weight averaging of recent checkpoints is the scalable version of this.
  • Continual self-supervised training forgets tail regimes unless you make it not forget: cross-version representation distillation (CaSSLe-style predictor from new to old latent space) plus replay of rare regimes doubles as the backward-compatibility mechanism.
  • Label delay makes reactive online learning adapt to stale concepts: in systems where outcomes arrive after decisions, adaptation must be proactive (drift-estimated parameter adjustment, meta-learned adaptation from unlabeled context) rather than fine-tuning on the latest labels (Proceed, ARM).
  • Unsupervised test-time adaptation is fragile in feedback-loop systems: entropy-minimization-style TTA suffers online batch dependency and impossible model selection (TTAB), and in a platform whose own surge policy shapes the data stream it can amplify performative drift — keep it out of the control loop.
  • Evaluate aging, not just accuracy: the decay of frozen-encoder probe performance as a function of train-serve gap (a 'future-decay curve') is the operational quantity that sets retraining cadence and is itself a release metric.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • Adopt Wild-Time's protocols as the encoder release contract: Eval-Fix (train on data through time T, gate the release on frozen-encoder linear-probe metrics over (T, T+delta]) for every version, and Eval-Stream (rolling next-K-buckets evaluation) in CI to tune cadence. Ban random temporal splits everywhere in the embedding pipeline, including probe-head model selection.
  • Make the future-decay curve a first-class release metric: for each release, freeze the encoder trained through T and plot each decision-relevant linear probe's metric at serve gaps of 1, 2, 4, 8, 12 weeks, per city; report the decay half-life. Set the retraining cadence per city where cumulative decay exceeds the downstream error budget — expect the Wild-Time-style ~20% cliff if this is never measured.
  • Default retraining policy: scheduled warm-start retrains (monthly is a sane start) from the previous checkpoint with shrink-and-perturb plus LR re-warming/re-decay, and a replay buffer over historical data with deliberate oversampling of tail regimes (extreme weather, holidays, outages, past surge-cap events) so continual training does not forget them. Reserve full from-scratch retrains for architecture changes or when the warm-start lineage's Eval-Fix metrics degrade versus a from-scratch challenger trained annually.
  • Do not adopt DRAIN-style full-parameter forecasting for the production encoder: it scales poorly and churns latent geometry, breaking backward compatibility. If you want future-proofing between retrains, evaluate TEA-style trajectory-aware weight averaging of the last k checkpoints against the 'latest checkpoint' baseline on Eval-Stream — it is cheap, works at full model scale, and weight averaging keeps successive versions geometrically close.
  • Two-speed architecture (answers question 4): keep the trunk frozen between releases and designate STID-style per-hex/per-time-slot identity embeddings plus normalization/affine layers as the fast-adapting component, refreshed daily-to-weekly with a small learning rate and an L2 tether to their previous values (bounding drift so downstream consumers see smooth movement). Monitor identity-embedding displacement per hex as a cheap spatially-resolved drift telemetry signal.
  • Add CaSSLe-style cross-version distillation to every warm-start retrain: a small predictor maps new embeddings to the previous version's space, trained jointly with the SSL/auxiliary objectives. This simultaneously (a) mitigates representation forgetting under continual training and (b) yields the alignment map the versioning workstream needs for backward compatibility — implement it once, own it jointly.
  • Prohibit Tent-style unsupervised test-time adaptation inside the serving loop: entropy minimization on a data stream your own surge policy shapes risks confidence collapse and performative feedback (TTAB failure modes). If serve-time adaptation is needed, use mechanisms validated offline: ARM-style meta-learned adaptation from an unlabeled context window of the current market, or Proceed-style drift-to-parameter-offset generators trained on historical drift pairs — both respect label delay by not requiring fresh labels.
  • Operationally disentangle environment drift from policy-induced drift (question 3): log policy version, surge parameters, and experiment flags as first-class fields in the training corpus; maintain frozen-policy shadow evaluations (holdout zones or switchback windows) so probe decay can be attributed. Trigger scheduled warm-start retrains on environment drift (decay curves degrade under frozen policy too); on policy changes, do NOT trigger an immediate encoder retrain — the encoder should be action-conditioned per the causal workstream, and post-change data should be folded in only after adequate state-action coverage exists, otherwise the encoder memorizes the new policy rather than the market.
  • Sequence the evidence before adding TDG complexity: run a bake-off on your own Eval-Stream between (a) latest warm-started checkpoint, (b) TEA-style averaged checkpoints, (c) prompting/identity-embedding fast adaptation, and (d) a FreKoo-style low-frequency-only extrapolation regularizer. The Reality Check result predicts (a) is hard to beat — require a sustained win across at least two cities and two quarters before promoting anything fancier to production.
  • For small per-zone tabular probe models (not the main encoder), pilot Drift-Resilient TabPFN-style in-context adaptation: it is the only tabular approach shown to consistently beat drift-ignoring supervised learning and needs no retraining infrastructure, making it a low-cost hedge for long-tail cities with sparse data.

State of the art

Continuous-time representation learning treats the raw data — an asynchronous, marked event stream on an evolving graph — as the object to encode, rather than pre-aggregating it into per-zone-per-bucket tensors. The field grew from two roots that merged around 2019-2020: (1) neural temporal point processes (Neural Hawkes 2017, Transformer Hawkes 2020), which model the conditional intensity lambda(t) of the event process and thus represent 'demand' as an arrival rate rather than a count — the principled framing under censoring, since a censored likelihood can recover latent demand suppressed by price or supply caps; and (2) continuous-time dynamic graph embeddings (DyRep 2019, JODIE 2019, TGAT 2020, unified by TGN 2020), which maintain per-node embeddings updated event-by-event, with JODIE's projection operator making the embedding queryable at ANY timestamp between updates. Since 2023 the field has been reshaped by rigorous benchmarking (Temporal Graph Benchmark, NeurIPS 2023) which exposed that on dynamic node-property prediction — the task closest to estimating a zone's market state — simple heuristics and memory-free sequence models (DyGFormer 2023, CRAFT 2025) often match or beat complex memory-based models at a fraction of the serving cost; industrial frameworks (TGL at VLDB 2022, LasTGL 2023) show billion-edge training is feasible but confirms that per-node memory imposes sequential-update and state-management burdens. Open problems most relevant here: principled evaluation beyond link prediction, long-range dependency modeling, action-conditioned/counterfactual intensity under policy-endogenous logging, and how stateful memories interact with model versioning. The pragmatic 2026 consensus is a hybrid: heavy event-level encoding offline or as auxiliary training signal, cheap stateless queries online — not wholesale replacement of aggregated pipelines.

Key principles

  • Make time a first-class input, not an index: functional time encodings (TGAT's Bochner features, THP's temporal encodings) let one embedding be a queryable function of continuous time, eliminating the bucket width as a hand-engineered hyperparameter at the representation level even if serving stays bucketed.
  • There are exactly two mechanisms for any-timestamp queryability, with opposite ops profiles: (a) stored state + learned projection/decay (JODIE, TGN memory) — O(1) at query time but introduces version-specific mutable state; (b) stateless recomputation over a recent event window (TGAT, DyGFormer, CRAFT) — pure function of data, replayable and version-portable, at higher per-query compute. Choose per consumer latency budget.
  • Likelihood of the event process (TPP intensity) is the principled training signal for demand: it models the generative arrival process rather than a bucketed statistic, supports censored likelihoods that recover demand suppressed by the surge lever, and conditioning the intensity on the action gives an action-conditioned representation — directly addressing policy-endogeneity of logged data.
  • Auxiliary event-level objectives transfer even when serving stays aggregated: DyRep's core insight — parameterize event intensities BY the embedding — means a bucketed encoder trained with an intensity head is forced to encode the underlying arrival dynamics without any event-level serving infrastructure.
  • Benchmark before believing: TGB shows complex temporal-graph models often lose to persistence/moving-average baselines on node property prediction (the task class closest to zone market state), and rankings flip across datasets — event-level lift must be demonstrated on the company's own downstream tasks, not assumed.
  • Memory-free is winning on the efficiency-accuracy frontier (DyGFormer 2023, CRAFT 2025): per-node memories create sequential update dependencies, train-serve skew risk, and version-coupled state; recent-history attention restores parallelism and determinism.
  • Split compute offline-heavy / online-cheap: expensive encoding of long event histories offline (or in training-time auxiliary heads), cheap projection or short-window recomputation online — the same pattern as BigST in the spatiotemporal survey, mirrored by JODIE's stored-trajectory + projection design.

Recommendations for the market-state embedding project

  • BUILD NOW — TPP intensity auxiliary head on the bucketed encoder (answers Q2: yes, and it is the highest value-per-cost item in this survey). Add a head that models the conditional intensity of request/driver-session arrivals within each zone as a function of the market-state latent z AND the concurrently active surge action a: lambda(t | z, a). Use a censored likelihood (Gammelli et al.) so bookings suppressed or abandoned under high surge are treated as censored observations of latent demand, not as low demand. This forces the latent to encode the un-censored arrival process and its price sensitivity — the action-conditioned, decision-relevant structure that reconstruction loss cannot induce. Architecture: THP-style attention head or a Shchur-style mixture-density head (closed-form likelihood, no numerical integration) to keep training cheap.
  • PILOT — stateless event-window encoder as an input pathway (answers Q1: bucketed aggregates are a strong baseline; event-level wins are task- and regime-dependent). Replace hand-binned within-bucket counts with a DyGFormer/CRAFT-style transformer over the last N raw events per zone (with Bochner time encodings), feeding the bucketed spine. Evaluate strictly against the persistence and bucketed-count baselines per TGB's findings, on downstream linear-probe metrics, and expect gains concentrated in bursty/fine-timescale regimes (event onset, surge transitions) rather than uniformly. Budget per TGL: full event-level training at billions of events needs dedicated infrastructure — use LasTGL/TGL-style tooling rather than a bespoke stack for the pilot.
  • BUILD — JODIE-style time-of-query projection on the served embedding (answers Q3: yes, with a caveat). Store per-zone state at each refresh tick plus a learned drift/decay head g(z, delta_t) so any consumer can request the embedding at its own timestamp; this serves heterogeneous freshness needs (dispatch wants seconds-fresh, forecasting is fine with the tick) from one artifact with no extra refresh infrastructure. Caveat: projection accuracy decays with elapsed time — publish the measured staleness-vs-accuracy curve as part of the embedding contract so consumers can decide whether projected values meet their SLO.
  • AVOID for the market-state backbone — per-entity TGN memory (answers Q4). Memory vectors are encoder-version-specific hidden state: they are meaningless under a new encoder version and must be rebuilt by replaying a bounded event history at every version bump, complicating the backward-compatibility contract and creating train-serve skew risk. Prefer memory-free designs where the embedding is a deterministic function of (recent event window, model weights): version migration is then just re-running the new encoder over the same window, and shadow-serving two versions is trivial. If a memory-based component is ever adopted, mandate (a) a bounded replay window sufficient to reconstruct memory from raw events, and (b) replay-based warm-up as part of the version-rollout runbook.
  • NET BUILD/SKIP VERDICT: build the event-level layer as TRAINING SIGNAL and INPUT PATHWAY, skip it as SERVING BACKBONE. Concretely: (1) action-conditioned censored TPP intensity head — build now; (2) stateless per-zone event-window encoder feeding the bucketed spine — pilot behind an ablation gate; (3) time-of-query projection on served embeddings — build; (4) full per-entity continuous-time dynamic-graph system (memories over all drivers/riders) — skip for market state; revisit only for entity-level products (driver churn, fraud, matching affinity) where per-entity trajectories are the actual object of interest.
  • Evaluation add-ons: adopt TGB-style protocol discipline (temporal splits, strong naive baselines, node-property tasks) for the event-level pilot, and use the EasyTPP benchmark toolkit (ICLR 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.08097) for standardized TPP-head goodness-of-fit checks; report intensity calibration under held-out surge levels to verify the action-conditioning actually generalizes off-policy.

2021 Wager, S., Xu, K. (2021). Experimenting in Equilibrium. Management Science 67(11). arXiv:1903.02124.
arxiv.org/abs/1903.02124

Designs experiments for large stochastic systems with cross-unit interference captured by mean-field modeling: instead of A/B splitting units, the platform applies small random local perturbations ('dithering') to a global lever (e.g., supply-side incentives on a ride-sharing platform) while the system stays in equilibrium. Combining this unobtrusive randomization with lightweight structural modeling yields consistent estimates of the derivative of steady-state outcomes with respect to the lever — the marginal policy effect — which naive A/B comparisons get wrong under interference.

Relevance to this project: Gives the mathematically grounded recipe for the permanent lever randomization the project needs so that surge dose-response is identifiable from logs, and shows why zone-randomized A/Bs of pricing are biased in equilibrium — directly shaping both logging spec and causal-head training data.

2022 Johari, R., Li, H., Liskovich, I., Weintraub, G.Y. (2022). Experimental Design in Two-Sided Platforms: An Analysis of Bias. Management Science 68(10). arXiv:2002.05670.
arxiv.org/pdf/2002.05670

Builds a stochastic two-sided market model (customers arriving to a shared, limited supply of listings/drivers) and analytically characterizes the bias of customer-side, listing-side, and two-sided randomized experiments when treatment changes market balance. Shows bias can be as large as the treatment effect itself and that two-sided randomization reduces but does not eliminate it, with bias formulas depending on market tightness.

Relevance to this project: The canonical statement of why naive A/B tests on a ride-hailing/delivery platform mis-measure intervention effects — any evaluation of embedding-driven pricing/dispatch policies, and any engagement-supervised training label, inherits this bias unless corrected.

2023 Bojinov, I., Simchi-Levi, D., Zhao, J. (2023). Design and Analysis of Switchback Experiments. Management Science 69(7). arXiv:2009.00148.
arxiv.org/pdf/2009.00148

Formalizes switchback experiments — randomly alternating a whole market between treatment and control over time — under carryover effects of bounded order, casting optimal switch-interval design as a minimax discrete optimization and solving it via continuous relaxation, with exact randomization-based inference. Provides the standard design/analysis toolkit used by ride-hailing and delivery platforms for market-level interventions where unit-level randomization is impossible.

Relevance to this project: Switchbacks are the workhorse for generating exogenous variation in the surge lever at market level; the resulting logs are exactly the action-randomized data the action-conditioned encoder heads and OPE scorecards require.

2022 Farias, V., Li, A.A., Peng, T., Zheng, A. (2022). Markovian Interference in Experiments. NeurIPS 2022 (oral). arXiv:2206.02371.
arxiv.org/abs/2206.02371

Models experiments in dynamical systems where interventions on some units affect others through a shared state (e.g., limited driver inventory) as a Markov decision process, and proposes the Differences-In-Q's (DQ) estimator: estimate Q-functions from logged data and difference them between treatment and control states. DQ has bias second-order in the intervention size but exponentially smaller variance than full off-policy evaluation, empirically dominating naive and OPE estimators in a ride-hailing simulator.

Relevance to this project: Connects the project's learned value/Q auxiliary heads directly to unbiased-ish experiment readouts under interference: the same embedding-based Q heads used for dispatch can power DQ estimation of new-policy and new-embedding-version effects.

2022 Luo, S., Yang, Y., Shi, C., Yao, F., Ye, J., Zhu, H. (2022, rev. 2023). Policy Evaluation for Temporal and/or Spatial Dependent Experiments. arXiv:2202.10887.
arxiv.org/abs/2202.10887

Introduces a Varying Coefficient Decision Process model for experiments run in ride-sourcing markets where outcomes depend on treatments at other times and neighboring regions; treatment effects are decomposed into direct and indirect (spillover) components with estimation and inference procedures, validated on simulations and real ride-hailing applications.

Relevance to this project: This is exactly the project's setting — spatially and temporally dependent lever experiments in ride-hailing — and its direct/indirect decomposition defines the estimands the spillover-aware auxiliary heads should target.

2025 Zhu, J., Li, J., Zhou, H., Lin, Y., Lin, Z., Shi, C. (2025). Balancing Interference and Correlation in Spatial Experimental Designs: A Causal Graph Cut Approach. ICML 2025. arXiv:2505.20130.
arxiv.org/abs/2505.20130

Optimizes the assignment of spatial units to treatment clusters by minimizing a surrogate of the MSE of the global treatment effect estimator via graph-cut algorithms, jointly trading off interference across cluster boundaries against outcome correlation within clusters, and accommodating moderate-to-large interference and general covariance structures. Relevance to this project: State-of-the-art 2025 answer to 'how should we partition hexes into experiment clusters for surge experiments' — the design layer that determines the quality of all causal training signal on the zone graph.

2022 Ma, H., Fang, F., Parkes, D.C. (2022). Spatio-Temporal Pricing for Ridesharing Platforms. Operations Research 70(2). arXiv:1801.04015.

Develops the Spatio-Temporal Pricing (STP) mechanism in a discrete-time, multi-location model of a ridesharing market: prices computed from a minimum-cost-flow planning problem are smooth in space and time, making it a subgame-perfect equilibrium for strategic drivers to accept dispatches rather than chase surge into neighboring zones; the mechanism is welfare-optimal, envy-free, and budget balanced from any history onward.

Relevance to this project: Supplies the structural/economic model of WHY surge creates spatial spillovers (driver strategic repositioning) and what smoothness constraints prevent them — grounding for simulator-based counterfactual training data and for spatial-smoothness regularization of lever-response heads.

2019 Kumar, S., Zhang, X., Leskovec, J. (2019). Predicting Dynamic Embedding Trajectory in Temporal Interaction Networks (JODIE). KDD 2019, pp. 1269-1278.
